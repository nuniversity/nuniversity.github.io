# Non-Profit Accounting and Financial Management

Proper accounting and financial management are essential for non-profit transparency, regulatory compliance, and stakeholder trust. This lesson covers the unique accounting principles and practices that distinguish non-profits from for-profits.

## Fundamental Accounting Principles

### 1. Fund Accounting
- **Core concept**: Resources segregated into self-balancing funds based on restrictions or purpose
- **Why needed**: Distinguish between restricted (donor-limited) and unrestricted funds
- **Primary funds**: 
  - **Unrestricted**: Board-determined or general operating purposes
  - **Temporarily restricted**: Subject to donor-imposed time or purpose conditions
  - **Permanently restricted** (endowments): Principal must be maintained in perpetuity; only income may be spent

### 2. Statement of Activities (Income Statement)
- **Format**: Revenue - Expenses = Change in Net Assets
- **Net assets without donor restrictions**: Equivalent to "equity" in for-profit
- **Net assets with donor restrictions**: Subject to donor conditions
- **Changes during period**: Additions and deductions affecting each net asset class

### 3. Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet)
- **Assets**: Cash, investments, receivables, prepaid expenses, fixed assets
- **Liabilities**: Accounts payable, accrued liabilities, long-term debt
- **Net assets**: Total assets minus total liabilities (split with/without donor restrictions)
- **Equation**: Assets = Liabilities + Net Assets

### 4. Statement of Cash Flows
- **Operating activities**: Cash received from contributions, payments to employees/ vendors
- **Investing activities**: Purchase/sale of investments, purchase of fixed assets
- **Financing activities**: Borrowing, repayment of debt, contribution of principal to endowments

### 5. Statement of Functional Expenses
- **Unique to non-profits**: Allocates expenses by function (program, management, fundraising)
- **Purpose**: Demonstrate mission focus; required by many donors and regulators
- **Allocation methods**: Direct charges, apportionment based on headcount, square footage, or activity drivers

## Chart of Accounts Structure

### Asset Accounts
- **Cash and cash equivalents**: Operating checking, petty cash
- **Short-term investments**: Marketable securities maturing within 1 year
- **Accounts receivable**: Grants receivable, fee-for-service receivables
- **Pledges receivable**: Promised donations not yet received
- **Prepaid expenses**: Insurance, rent, subscriptions paid in advance
- **Fixed assets**: Buildings, equipment, vehicles (net of depreciation)
- **Accumulated depreciation**: Total depreciation taken over asset life

### Liability Accounts
- **Accounts payable**: Unpaid bills, vendor invoices
- **Accrued expenses**: Salaries, taxes, interest accrued but unpaid
- **Grant liabilities**: Unearned revenue from advance government funding
- **Accounts payable - restricted**: Liabilities restricted by donor
- **Long-term debt**: Mortgages, loans, bonds payable

### Net Asset Accounts
- **Net assets without donor restrictions**: Board-designated, general operating
- **Net assets with donor restrictions**: Time/purpose restrictions; expires when conditions met
- **Net assets from prior period**: Beginning balance for the reporting period

### Revenue Accounts
- **Contributions - unrestricted**: Individual, corporate donations without restrictions
- **Contributions - temporarily restricted**: Time or purpose-restricted gifts
- **Contributions - permanently restricted**: Endowment principal gifts
- **Government grants**: Restricted or unrestricted government funding
- **Fee-for-service revenue**: Program fees, tuition, sales of goods
- **Investment income**: Interest, dividends, endowment spending
- **Special events revenue**: Gala, auction, charity event proceeds (net of direct costs)

### Expense Accounts (Functional)
- **Program expenses**: Direct costs of mission activities
- **Management and general**: Executive leadership, finance, legal, HR
- **Fundraising**: Development staff, events, campaigns, donor relations

## Financial Statements Preparation

### Annual Reporting Cycle
1. **Collect financial data** from accounting system throughout the year
2. **Reconcile all accounts** monthly; resolve discrepancies
3. **Classify revenues and expenses** by net asset category and function
4. **Prepare draft financial statements** for review
5. **External audit** (if required; many non-profits above revenue thresholds)
6. **Form 990 preparation** (US) or equivalent regulatory filing
7. **Board review and approval** of annual financial statements
8. **Public disclosure** as required (Form 990 available publicly; many non-profits publish summary)

### Form 990 (US Example) Key Sections
- **Part I**: Summary of revenues, expenses, and net assets
- **Part II**: Statement of accomplishments (mission narrative)
- **Part III**: Grants and other amounts paid to or for organizations
- **Part IV**: Business transactions involving officers, directors, trustees
- **Part V**: Statement of revenue supporting the organization's exempt purpose
- **Part VI**: Governance, management, and disclosure
- **Part VII**: Compensation of officers, directors, trustees, key employees
- **Part VIII**: Statement of revenue from related entities
- **Part IX**: Statement of functional expenses
- **Part X**: Balance sheet
- **Part XI**: Reconciliation of net assets
- **Part XII**: Budgetary program

## Compliance and Regulatory Requirements

### Annual Filing Requirements (US)
- **Form 990**: Annual information return (most non-profits with $50,000+ gross receipts)
- **Form 990-EZ**: Short form for organizations with gross receipts < $200,000
- **Form 990-N**: e-Postcard for very small non-profits (gross receipts ≤ $50,000)
- **State filings**: Vary by state; may include annual reports, charity registration renewals
- **Loss of tax-exempt status**: Failure to file for 3 consecutive years triggers automatic revocation

### Audit Requirements
- **Automatic trigger**: Generally non-profits with ≥ $500,000 in total revenues
- **Grantor requirements**: Some government grants require audits regardless of size
- **Donor requirements**: Major foundations may require audited financials for large grants
- **Single audit**: Required for non-profits spending ≥ $750,000 in federal awards (Uniform Guidance)

### Key Compliance Areas

| Area | Requirement | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Form 990** | Annual information return | Annual |
| **Audit** | Independent CPA audit | Annually (if threshold met) |
| **990-T** | Unrelated business income tax return | As needed |
| **State charity registration** | Renewal + annual reporting | Annual |
| **Payroll taxes** | Withholding, remittance | Per pay cycle |
| **Sales tax** | Exemption certificates, filing | Varies by jurisdiction |
| **Employment laws** | Wage/hour, safety, discrimination | Ongoing + as changes occur |

## Financial Ratio Analysis

### Liquidity Ratios
| Ratio | Formula | Benchmark | Action if Outside |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Current Ratio** | Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities | 1.5 - 3.0 | < 1.0: liquidity crisis risk |
| **Days Cash on Hand** | Cash ÷ (Total expenses ÷ 12) | 3-6 months | < 3 months: tight liquidity |
| **Operating Reserve** | Unrestricted cash ÷ Monthly operating expenses | 3-6 months | < 1 month: risk exposure |

### Efficiency Ratios
| Ratio | Formula | Benchmark | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Expense Ratio** | Program expenses ÷ Total expenses | < 75% (varies) | Lower = more mission-focused (but not at quality's expense) |
| **Program Efficiency** | Program services ÷ Total expenses | 70-85% | Proportion supporting direct mission |
| **Management/G breakdown** | Mgmt + General ÷ Total | 10-15% | Too high may indicate overadmin; too low may indicate under-governance |

### Sustainability Ratios
| Ratio | Formula | Benchmark | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue Diversification** | Number of significant funding sources | ≥ 3-5 | More diversified = more stable |
| **Reserve Ratio** | Unrestricted cash ÷ Monthly expenses | 3-6 months | Measures ability to withstand downturns |
| **Fundraising ROI** | Net revenue raised ÷ Fundraising costs | > 1:1 or as defined | Positive return on fundraising investment |

### Stewardship Ratios
| Ratio | Formula | Benchmark | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Donor Retention Rate** | Returning donors ÷ Prior year donors | 40-45% (varies) | Key indicator of donor satisfaction |
| **Average Donation Size** | Total donations ÷ Number of gifts | Varies by organization | Track trends over time |
| **Matching Gift Participation** | Employees using matching gifts | Varies | Indicates organizational culture |

## Common Accounting Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

### 1. Misclassifying Restricted vs Unrestricted Funds
- **Problem**: Commingling; inaccurate reporting; donor disputes
- **Fix**: Clear policies at intake; coded accounting system; regular training for staff

### 2. Inadequate Documentation for Grants
- **Problem**: Cannot demonstrate compliance; risk future funding; audit findings
- **Fix**: Grant-specific tracking; regular progress reports; documentation retention policy (7+ years)

### 3. Missing Filing Deadlines
- **Problem**: Late fees; loss of tax-exempt status; reputational damage
- **Fix**: Calendar with all deadlines; reminders; assigned responsibility; backup person

### 4. Capital Asset Mismanagement
- **Problem**: Not capitalizing properly; incorrect depreciation; misstated assets
- **Fix**: Capitalization policy (threshold, useful life); depreciation schedule; annual physical inventory

### 5. Budget vs Actual Variances Not Analyzed
- **Problem**: Repeated over/under spending; no corrective action; board blind spot
- **Fix**: Monthly variance analysis; quarterly board financial review; explanations for significant variances

### 6. Inadequate Internal Controls
- **Problem**: Fraud risk; errors; lack of segregation of duties
- **Fix**: Written policies; dual signatures for large expenditures; regular internal audits; whistleblower policy

## Best Practices for Non-Profit Financial Management

### 1. Board Financial Oversight
- **Financial committee**: Standing committee of board responsible for financial oversight
- **Monthly financial reports**: Presented to entire board with variance analysis
- **Annual budget approval**: Board adopts budget before fiscal year start
- **Form 990 review**: Board reviews before filing; ensures accuracy and completeness

### 2. Staff Financial Competency
- **Financial training**: Staff involved in finance receive ongoing professional development
- **Clear role definitions**: Who can approve expenditures; who prepares reports
- **Software proficiency**: Accounting software (QuickBooks, Sage, Non-profit-specific systems)
- **Regular skill updates**: As regulations and best practices evolve

### 3. Transparency and Disclosure
- **Public Form 990**: Make available upon request; many post on website
- **Financial summary for donors**: One-page overview of health and impact
- **Annual report**: Combines financials with program outcomes and stories
- **Website disclosures**: As required by state law; enhancing credibility

### 4. Technology and Systems
- **Non-profit-specific accounting software**: QuickBooks Non-Profit, Blackbaud, NetSuite Non-Profit
- **Donor management**: Integrated with accounting for complete financial picture
- **Grant management**: Track restrictions, reporting deadlines, fund utilization
- **Budgeting tools**: Scenario planning; what-if analysis for different funding scenarios

### 5. External Relationships
- **CPA firm**: Non-profit specialist for audits, tax returns, advisory
- **Banker**: Understands non-profit cash flow patterns and lending
- **Insurance broker**: Coverage tailored to non-profit risks (D&O, liability, property)
- **Legal counsel**: On governance, compliance, employment, contract issues
