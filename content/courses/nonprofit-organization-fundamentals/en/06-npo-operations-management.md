# Non-Profit Operations and Management

Effective operations and management ensure that non-profit organizations can deliver their mission efficiently, sustainably, and with appropriate quality. This lesson covers the key operational disciplines needed for third-sector success.

## Operational Planning and Design

### 1. Mission-Focused Operational Planning
- **Strategic alignment**: Every operational decision connects back to organizational mission
- **Annual operational plan**: Translates strategic goals into yearly objectives and actions
- **Key result areas**: Identify 3-5 critical areas where operations must excel for mission success
- **Resource allocation**: Deploy staff, funds, and facilities to priority areas first

### 2. Program Management Lifecycle

| Phase | Activities | Key Questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Design** | Needs assessment; design program theory; logic model | Who needs what? What change do we seek? |
| **Implementation** | Hire staff; procure materials; launch activities | Are we executing as designed? |
| **Monitoring** | Track attendance; record outputs; regular check-ins | Are we doing what we planned? |
| **Evaluation** | Collect outcome data; compare to goals; assess impact | Are we achieving our intended change? |
| **Adaptation** | Modify activities based on findings; scale what works | What's working? What needs change? |

### 3. Workflow Design and Process Improvement

#### Process Mapping
- **Visual workflow**: Document each step from input to output
- **Identify bottlenecks**: Where do delays, errors, or inefficiencies occur?
- **Stakeholder involvement**: Include staff, volunteers, beneficiaries in mapping
- **Simplify and automate**: Remove non-value-added steps; use technology where appropriate

#### Common Non-Profit Processes
- **Volunteer onboarding**: Application → training → assignment → supervision → recognition
- **Grant management**: Application → award → reporting → fund disbursement → closeout
- **Volunteer scheduling**: Shift sign-up → assignment → reminder → tracking → evaluation
- **Donor management**: Prospect → cultivation → solicitation → stewardship → renewal

#### Process Improvement Methodologies
- **Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA)**: Test changes on small scale; learn; scale what works
- **Six Sigma**: Reduce defects/variation; data-driven quality improvement
- **Lean methodology**: Eliminate waste; optimize flow; maximize value
- **Continuous quality improvement (CQI)**: Ongoing, incremental improvements

## Volunteer Coordination and Management

### 1. Volunteer Roles and Classification
- **Direct service**: Working with beneficiaries (tutoring, mentoring, direct care)
- **Indirect service**: Supporting operations (admin, events, marketing)
- **Governance**: Board members, committee chairs
- **Skilled volunteering**: Professionals donating expertise (pro bono legal, accounting, IT)
- **Virtual volunteering**: Remote tasks (social media, research, translation)

### 2. Volunteer Lifecycle
```
Recruitment → Screening → Onboarding → Training → Assignment → Supervision → Recognition → Transition → Offboarding
```

### 3. Key Volunteer Management Practices

| Practice | Description | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear role descriptions** | Specific expectations, time commitment, skills needed | Write before recruitment; post on website; discuss in interview |
| **Structured onboarding** | Orientation, training, resources, introductions | Half-day or multi-session; include mission, policies, systems |
| **Regular supervision** | Scheduled check-ins; feedback; support | Weekly or bi-weekly for direct service; monthly for indirect |
| **Skill development** | Training opportunities; progression paths | Offer workshops; conference attendance; mentorship programs |
| **Recognition and appreciation** | Acknowledge contribution; celebrate milestones | Thank-you notes; volunteer appreciation events; annual awards; simple "thank you" |
| **Volunteer feedback** | Collect input; act on suggestions | Surveys; exit interviews; suggestion boxes; regular check-ins |
| **Volunteer agreements** | Commitment expectations; policies; liability coverage | Written agreement signed by volunteer and organization |

### 4. Balancing Volunteers and Paid Staff
- **Volunteer advantage**: Mission-driven, cost-effective, community connections
- **Paid staff advantage**: Consistency, professional expertise, employment benefits, reliability
- **Optimal mix**: Varies by organization size, budget, program type
- **Transition guidelines**: When to convert volunteer role to paid; when to create new volunteer role

## Risk Management and Compliance

### 1. Types of Risk in Non-Profits

| Risk Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Program risk** | Harm to beneficiaries from program activities | Safety incidents; inadequate screening; poor quality control |
| **Financial risk** | Misappropriation of funds; budget overruns; fraud | Embezzlement; unallowed expenses; poor internal controls |
| **Reputational risk** | Damage to organization's standing/credibility | Scandal; mismanagement allegations; failed programs |
| **Legal/Compliance risk** | Violation of laws/regulations; loss of tax-exempt status | Non-filing; regulatory violations; employment law breaches |
| **Operational risk** | Disruption to operations; inability to deliver | Natural disasters; system failures; key staff departure |
| **Volunteer/employee risk** | Harm or liability from people | Abuse; negligence; workers' compensation issues |

### 2. Internal Control Framework

#### Control Environment
- **Tone at the top**: Board and leadership set emphasis on integrity, ethics, compliance
- **Written policies**: Formal documents covering key operational areas
- **Board oversight**: Committee structure; regular financial and program review
- **Staff accountability**: Clear roles; performance expectations; consequences

#### Key Controls
- **Segregation of duties**: No one person controls entire transaction cycle
  - Example: Person who authorizes payments ≠ person who records transactions ≠ person who reconciles accounts
- **Approval thresholds**: Who can approve what amount of expenditure
- **Reconciliation**: Regular bank/ledger reconciliation (monthly recommended)
- **Documentation**: Receipts, contracts, agreements for all significant transactions
- **Access controls**: System passwords; physical key control; signature authority limits

#### Monitoring and Review
- **Internal audits**: Regular self-assessment of controls (annually or as needed)
- **External audits**: Independent CPA examination (when required or best practice)
- **Board financial committee**: Review of financial reports; variance analysis; policy compliance
- **Key performance indicators**: Dashboards monitoring operational health metrics

### 3. Insurance and Liability

| Coverage Type | Purpose | Typical Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **General liability** | Bodily injury/property damage to third parties | $1-5M per occurrence; $2-10M aggregate |
| **Directors and Officers (D&O)** | Protect board/officers from personal liability | Employment practices; wrongful acts; regulatory actions |
| **Professional liability** | Coverage for professional services errors/omissions | Errors & omissions; malpractice (for counselors, therapists, consultants) |
| **Cyber liability** | Data breach; privacy violations; digital extortion | First-party (organization's costs) + third-party (claims by others) |
| **Workers' compensation** | Employee injury/illness on the job | Required by law in most jurisdictions; medical expenses + wage replacement |
| **Volunteer liability** | Coverage for volunteer actions/incidents | Often included in general liability; verify coverage limits |
| **Property insurance** | Building, equipment, inventory damage/loss | Replacement cost; actual cash value; business interruption |

### 4. Compliance with Laws and Regulations

| Area | Key Requirements | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| **Employment law** | Wage/hour (FLSA); anti-discrimination (Title VII, ADA, ADEA); workplace safety (OSHA) | Department of Labor; EEOC; OSHA |
| **Tax compliance** | Form 990 filing; unrelated business income tax (Form 990-T); payroll taxes | IRS; state tax authorities |
| **Fundraising registration** | Register in states where soliciting; annual reporting | State charity regulators; varies by state |
| **Data protection** | GDPR (EU), CCPA (California), other privacy laws | Regulatory bodies; private rights of action |
| **Accessibility** | Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA); website accessibility | Lawsuits; DOJ enforcement; private actions |
| **Sector-specific regulations** | Healthcare (HIPAA), education (Title IX), fundraising rules | Sector regulators; varies by sector and jurisdiction |

## Technology Use in Non-Profits

### 1. Essential Technology Systems

| System Category | Purpose | Popular Options |
|---|---|---|
| **Financial software** | Accounting, budgeting, reporting | QuickBooks Non-Profit; Sage Intacct; NetSuite |
| **Donor management** | Track donors, gifts, interactions | Bloomerang; Little Green Light; DonorPerfect |
| **Program management** | Track participants, outcomes, services | Breeze; Kindful; Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud |
| **Volunteer management** | Schedule, track, communicate volunteers | VolunteerHub; Galaxy Digital; signNow |
| **Email marketing** | Campaigns, newsletters, donor communication | Mailchimp; Constant Contact; Sendinblue |
| **Website/CMS** | Online presence; donation platform; content | WordPress; Drupal; Squarespace |
| **Collaboration** | Team communication; file sharing | Slack; Microsoft Teams; Google Workspace |

### 2. Digital Transformation Considerations

#### Assessing Readiness
- **Current state assessment**: What systems are in place? What's working/Not?
- **Staff digital literacy**: Skills gaps; training needs
- **Budget for technology**: One-time costs vs ongoing subscriptions; staff time
- **Organizational culture**: Openness to change; risk tolerance
- **Integration needs**: Systems must talk to each other; data silos

#### Common Pitfalls
- **Buying before defining process**: Technology automates inefficiency
- **Underestimating training**: Staff can't use new systems effectively
- **Integration challenges**: Data doesn't flow between systems; duplicate entry
- **Feature bloat**: Paying for unused functionality
- **Lack of governance**: No clear ownership; ad-hoc system use

#### Success Factors
- **Start with processes**: Design workflows before selecting technology
- **Phased implementation**: Begin with critical; add gradually
- **Staff involvement**: Include end-users in selection and implementation
- **Training and support**: Ongoing; not one-time; help desk availability
- **Measure and evaluate**: Track usage; impact on goals; adjust as needed

### 3. Data Security and Privacy

#### Best Practices
- **Password policies**: Strong passwords; multi-factor authentication; regular changes
- **Data encryption**: At rest and in transit for sensitive information
- **Access controls**: Least privilege principle; role-based access
- **Regular backups**: Test restoration; off-site/cloud backup
- **Incident response plan**: What to do in case of breach; communication plan
- **Privacy policy**: Posted on website; comply with applicable laws (GDPR, CCPA)
- **Data retention policy**: How long kept; secure destruction when no longer needed

#### Donor Data Protection
- **Secure payment processing**: PCI DSS compliance for credit card data
- **Consent management**: Explicit permission for communication; easy opt-out
- **Data minimization**: Collect only what's needed; don't hoard information
- **Third-party processors**: Vet vendors; ensure they have appropriate safeguards
- **Breach notification**: Know legal requirements; have communication templates
