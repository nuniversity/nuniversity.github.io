# Non-Profit Human Resources and Volunteer Management

Human capital is critical for non-profit success. This lesson covers strategies for recruiting, retaining, and managing both paid staff and volunteers who are essential to mission delivery.

## Strategic HR Planning for Non-Profits

### 1. HR Strategy Alignment with Mission
- **Competency framework**: Define the skills, knowledge, and attitudes needed for mission success
- **Job analysis**: For each role, identify responsibilities, required qualifications, reporting relationships
- **Strategic workforce plan**: Anticipate staffing needs based on strategic plan, program growth, turnover risks
- **Volunteer integration plan**: How volunteers complement/augment paid staff

### 2. Organizational Design Considerations

| Factor | Consideration | Impact on HR |
|---|---|---|
| **Size** | Staff count, budget, program scope | Determines HR infrastructure needs |
| **Geography** | Local, regional, national, international | Recruitment geography; relocation policies; labor law compliance |
| **Structure** | Flat vs hierarchical; centralized vs decentralized | Reporting lines; decision-making authority; communication flows |
| **Culture** | Mission-driven, entrepreneurial, collaborative | Recruitment messaging; onboarding; performance expectations |
| **Funding stability** | Diversified vs restricted funding | Salary predictability; hiring freeze risks; volunteer dependency |

### 3. Recruitment and Selection

#### Recruitment Channels for Mission-Driven Candidates
- **Specialized job boards**: Idealist.org, NonprofitJobs.org, Indeed with nonprofit filter
- **Professional associations**: Relevant field associations (education, health, environment)
- **University partnerships**: Career centers for recent graduates interested in sector
- **Board networks**: Board members' connections; alumni networks
- **Social media**: LinkedIn; Facebook groups; Twitter/X
- **Referrals**: Current staff, volunteers, donors often bring qualified candidates
- **Volunteer-to-staff pipelines**: Identify high-performing volunteers for paid roles

#### Selection Criteria Beyond Technical Skills
- **Mission alignment**: Articulated commitment to the organization's purpose
- **Resilience**: Ability to work with limited resources, navigate uncertainty
- **Adaptability**: Comfort with changing priorities, wearing multiple hats
- **Collaborative spirit**: Comfort working across functions; shared leadership
- **Cultural fit**: Alignment with organizational values; team dynamics

#### Interview Questions for Non-Profits
- "What motivates you to work in the non-profit sector versus for-profit or government?"
- "Describe a time you had to accomplish goals with limited resources."
- "How do you handle competing priorities when mission demands exceed available resources?"
- "What does 'working for mission' mean to you personally?"
- "How do you maintain your own motivation and avoid burnout in challenging work?"

### 4. Compensation and Benefits Strategy

#### Salary Considerations
- **Market research**: Use nonprofit salary surveys (Guidestar, Nonprofit HR, industry reports)
- **Geographic adjustments**: Cost-of-living differences across locations
- **Role complexity**: Responsibility breadth; level of autonomy; number of direct reports
- **Budget reality**: What can the organization afford sustainably?
- **Total compensation mindset**: Salary + benefits + mission fulfillment + experience values

#### Typical Non-Profit Compensation Structure

| Component | Typical Approach | Considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **Base salary** | Below for-profit equivalents for similar roles | Must be sustainable; benchmark regularly |
| **Benefits package** | Health insurance; retirement; PTO; professional development | Key retention tool; may be scaled to budget |
| **Stipends for advanced degrees** | Partial support for relevant education | Recoupment agreement if employee leaves within X years |
| **Performance bonuses** | Rare in non-profits; if used, tied to organizational + individual metrics | Must align with mission; avoid creating unintended incentives |
| **Mission bonus/values alignment** | Non-monetary recognition; mission fulfillment as intrinsic reward | Cultivate organizational culture that values mission |

#### Benefits Common in Non-Profits
- **Health insurance**: Often partial subsidy; professional association group rates
- **Retirement plans**: 403(b) instead of 401(k) (tax-sheltered annuity); may include employer match
- **Paid time off**: Typically 15-25 days annually, increasing with tenure
- **Professional development**: Conference attendance; course reimbursement; certification support
- **Flexible work arrangements**: Remote work; flexible hours; compressed workweeks (where feasible)
- **Employee assistance program (EAP)**: Counseling, financial advice, legal referrals (often EAP consortium with other non-profits)

### 5. Performance Management

#### Non-Profit Performance Review Framework

| Component | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal setting** | SMART goals aligned to role and organizational priorities | Annually, with mid-year check-in |
| **Core competency assessment** | Rating on defined competencies (technical, interpersonal, mission-alignment) | Annually |
| **Self-assessment** | Employee's own evaluation of performance | Annually, before manager review |
| **Manager evaluation** | Manager's rating and feedback on employee performance | Annually |
| **360-degree feedback** (optional) | Input from peers, direct reports, supervisors | Annually or semi-annually |
| **Development plan** | Identified areas for growth; training; stretch assignments | Created after review; updated quarterly |

#### Unique Non-Profit Performance Considerations

- **Mission impact**: How well does the role advance the organization's purpose?
- **Resourcefulness**: Ability to achieve results with limited means
- **Collaboration**: Working across departments, with volunteers, with partners
- **Sustainability awareness**: Operating within organizational means; avoiding burnout
- **Ethical conduct**: Adherence to organizational values; conflict-of-interest policies

#### Addressing Performance Issues
1. **Document specific concerns**: Behavioral observations, not characterizations
2. **Provide clear expectations**: What needs to improve; what good looks like
3. **Offer support**: Training; mentoring; workload adjustment; EAP referral
4. **Set improvement timeline**: 30/60/90-day plan with check-ins
5. **Consider reassignment**: Role may better suit strengths; or part ways professionally
6. **Follow employment procedures**: Consistent with policies; legal compliance; board involvement as needed

### 6. Volunteer Management Systematization

#### Volunteer Position Descriptions
- **Similar to job descriptions**: Roles, time commitment, responsibilities, skills needed
- **Differentiate from paid roles**: Emphasize flexibility; mission focus; no salary
- **Include supervision**: Who supervises; meeting frequency; support available
- **Outline impact**: What difference does this volunteer make?

#### Volunteer Recruitment Strategy
- **Multiple channels**: Volunteer matching websites (VolunteerMatch, Catch the Wave); social media; organizational website; word-of-mouth; community presentations
- **Clear asks**: Specific roles needed; time commitment; skills desired; impact of role
- **Targeted recruitment**: Match volunteer skills to organizational needs
- **Inclusive recruitment**: Remove barriers; multilingual materials; accessible processes

#### Volunteer Training and Development
- **Orientation**: Mission, policies, safety, key contacts, scope of role
- **Role-specific training**: Skills needed; procedures; resources; shadowing experienced volunteers
- **Ongoing education**: Workshops; conferences; webinars; peer learning groups
- **Leadership development**: For volunteers in coordination roles; succession planning

#### Volunteer Recognition and Retention
- **Immediate thanks**: Say thank you within 48 hours of contribution
- **Milestone recognition**: Acknowledge hours served; anniversaries; certifications
- **Public recognition**: Newsletter; annual report; social media (with permission)
- **Events**: Annual volunteer appreciation dinner; awards ceremony; casual gather
- **Impact storytelling**: Show volunteers the difference they make; share beneficiary stories
- **Leadership opportunities**: Promote from within; increase responsibility; mentor new volunteers

## Legal Compliance in HR

### 1. Employment Law Basics for Non-Profits

| Law | Key Requirement | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA)** | Minimum wage; overtime pay; child labor standards | Most employees; some volunteers depending on duties |
| **Title VII of Civil Rights Act** | Prohibits employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin | All employers with 15+ employees |
| **Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)** | Prohibits discrimination; requires reasonable accommodation | Employers with 15+ employees |
| **Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA)** | Prohibits discrimination against individuals 40+ | Employers with 20+ employees |
| **Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)** | Up to 12 weeks unpaid, job-protected leave | Employers with 50+ employees within 75 miles |
| **Occupational Safety and Health Act (OSHA)** | Safe working conditions; recordkeeping; employee right-to-know | Most employers; some volunteer situations |

### 2. Volunteer Protection and Legal Considerations

#### Volunteer Protection Act (US)
- **Liability protection**: For volunteers of non-profit and governmental organizations
- **Scope**: Acts of ordinary negligence (not gross negligence, willful misconduct, or crimes)
- **Limits**: Not protection if volunteer received compensation (beyond actual expenses); certain practice areas
- **State variations**: Some states have additional or different protections

#### Best Practices for Volunteer Management
- **Written role descriptions**: Define scope; set expectations
- **Screening procedures**: Applications; interviews; reference checks (especially for roles with vulnerable populations)
- **Training**: Role-specific; safety; policies; emergency procedures
- **Supervision**: Designated supervisor; regular check-ins
- **Liability coverage**: Verify insurance covers volunteer activities
- **Policy acknowledgment**: Volunteers sign receipt of key policies (harassment, code of conduct, confidentiality)

### 3. Compensation and Benefits Legal Considerations

#### Volunteers vs. Employees Classification
- **Economic reality test**: Consider control, investment, opportunity for profit/loss, skill required, duration, supervisory control
- **IRS 20-factor test**: Historical IRS classification criteria (now supplemented by economic reality test)
- **State variations**: Some states use ABC test or other criteria
- **Risk of misclassification**: Back taxes, benefits liability, penalties; legal counsel recommended

#### When Volunteers May Be Considered Employees
- **Excessive control** by organization over when/where/how work is done
- **Training provided** suggests employer-employee relationship
- **Work is core to organization's operations**
- **Full-time or regular part-time schedule**
- **Exclusive relationship** with one organization (vs. multiple)

### 4. Equal Employment Opportunity and Diversity

#### Affirmative Action Considerations
- **Voluntary vs. mandatory**: Depends on organization size, funding (federal contracts)
- **Benefits of diversity**: Broader perspectives; better community connection; improved program relevance
- **Recruitment strategies**: Expand outreach; remove biased screening criteria; diverse search committees
- **Retention initiatives**: Mentorship; Employee Resource Groups; inclusive culture assessment

#### Cultural Competency Development
- **Staff/board training**: Unconscious bias; cultural humility; language access
- **Language access**: Materials in communities' languages; interpreter services
- **Community advisory boards**: Input from beneficiaries; ensure accountability
- **Inclusive policies**: Holiday schedules; dress codes; communication styles
