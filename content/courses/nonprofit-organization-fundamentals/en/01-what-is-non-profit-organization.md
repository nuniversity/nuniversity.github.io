# What Is a Non-Profit Organization?

A **non-profit organization (NPO)** is a non-governmental entity that operates for a collective, public, or social benefit rather than to generate profit for private owners. Non-profit organizations are subject to a non-distribution constraint, meaning that any revenue exceeding expenses must be used to further the organization's purpose.

## Core Characteristics

- **Mission-driven**: Primary purpose is to achieve a social, educational, religious, or charitable objective
- **Non-distribution constraint**: Surplus revenues must be reinvested in the mission; cannot distribute profits to owners or directors
- **Non-governmental**: Independent from government control (though may receive government funding)
- **Accountability**: Responsible to donors, founders, volunteers, program recipients, and the public community
- **Tax-exempt status** (optional): In some jurisdictions, may qualify for tax-exempt status and tax-deductible contributions

## Legal Structures

Non-profits may take various legal forms depending on jurisdiction:

| Structure | Description | Key Features |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| **Trust** | Arrangement where trustees hold assets for beneficiaries | Common for foundations; flexible governance |
| **Association** | Membership-based organization with voluntary members | Democratic governance; members elect board |
| **Corporation** | Incorporated legal entity separate from members | Limited liability; can enter contracts, own property |
| **Cooperative** | Member-owned and -operated for mutual benefit | Member control; profit reinvested for members' benefit |

## Non-Profit vs For-Profit vs Government

| Aspect | Non-Profit | For-Profit | Government |
|--------|-----------|------------|------------|
| **Primary Purpose** | Public/social benefit | Generate profit for owners | Public service & governance |
| **Profit Distribution** | Reinvested in mission | Distributed to owners/shareholders | N/A (public funds) |
| **Ownership** | No private owners | Shareholders/owners | Citizens / public |
| **Accountability** | Donors, regulators, beneficiaries | Shareholders, customers | Voters, constitution |
| **Tax Status** | Possible tax-exempt | Pay taxes on profits | Tax-supported/appropriations |

## Formation and Governance

1. **File incorporation documents** (bylaws, articles of incorporation) with state/jurisdiction
2. **Obtain EIN** (Employer Identification Number) or equivalent tax ID
3. **Establish board of directors/trustees** with fiduciary duties
4. **Create bylaws** governing internal operations and decision-making
5. **Apply for tax-exempt status** if desired (e.g., 501(c)(3) in the US)

## Key Takeaways for Exam Day

> [!SUCCESS]
> **Key Takeaways:**
> 1. NPOs operate for collective/public benefit, not private profit
> 2. Surplus revenues must be reinvested in the mission (non-distribution constraint)
> 3. Legal structures include trust, association, corporation, or cooperative
> 4. Key distinction from for-profits: no profit distribution to owners
> 5. Accountable to donors, beneficiaries, regulators — not shareholders
