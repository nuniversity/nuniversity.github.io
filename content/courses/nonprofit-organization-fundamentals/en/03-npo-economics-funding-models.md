# Non-Profit Economics and Funding Models

Understanding the economic framework of non-profit organizations is critical for ensuring financial sustainability and mission impact. Unlike for-profits, which rely primarily on sales revenue, non-profits must diversify their funding sources to maintain operations.

## Core Economic Principles

### The Non-Distribution Constraint
- Surplus revenues must be reinvested in the organization's mission
- Cannot distribute profits to owners, directors, or members
- Creates unique economic constraints and opportunities
- Requires careful financial planning to balance mission and sustainability

### Double Bottom Line
- **Mission bottom line**: Achieving social/educational/charitable objectives
- **Financial bottom line**: Ensuring organizational sustainability and viability
- Both must be balanced for long-term success
- Mission focus without financial sustainability leads to organizational failure
- Financial focus without mission adherence leads to loss of purpose and credibility

## Revenue Sources

### 1. Individual Donations
- **Small-dollar donations**: Many contributors giving modest amounts
- **Major gifts**: Fewer contributors giving substantial amounts
- **Planned giving**: Bequests, estate gifts, life insurance policies
- **Average**: Typically 40-50% of non-profit revenue mix

### 2. Foundation Grants
- **Private foundations**: Established by individuals/families (e.g., Ford Foundation)
- **Community foundations**: Pool funds from local donors
- **Corporate foundations**: Philanthropic arms of corporations
- **Grant cycles**: Typically annual with reporting requirements
- **Average**: Typically 20-30% of revenue

### 3. Government Grants and Contracts
- **Project-specific funding**: For defined programs or services
- **Formula grants**: Based on formulas (population, need, etc.)
- **Contracts**: Payment for specified services delivered
- **Restricted vs unrestricted**: Most government funding is restricted
- **Average**: Typically 15-25% of revenue (varies significantly by sector)

### 4. Fee-for-Service Revenue
- **Program fees**: Charges for workshops, training, services
- **Sliding scale**: Fees based on ability to pay
- **Commercial activities**: Revenue-generating activities related to mission
- **Average**: Typically 10-20% of revenue

### 5. Investment Income
- **Endowment spending**: Annual draw from invested principal
- **Interest and dividends**: From investment portfolios
- **Capital gains**: From sale of investments
- **Average**: Typically 5-10% of revenue

### 6. Merchandise and Event Sales
- **Branded merchandise**: T-shirts, books, products related to mission
- **Special events**: Galas, galas, conferences, fundraisers
- **Membership dues**: Recurring fees for membership benefits
- **Average**: Typically 5-15% of revenue

## Funding Diversification Strategy

### Why Diversify?
- **Risk mitigation**: Reduce dependency on any single source
- **Seasonality**: Different sources have different cycles
- **Political changes**: Government funding may shift with administrations
- **Economic downturns**: Some sources more resilient than others
- **Donor fatigue**: Avoid over-reliance on the same donor base

### Diversification Approaches

| Approach | Description | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **Multiple donor types** | Individual, corporate, foundation, government | Develop strategy for each |
| **Multiple revenue streams** | Donations, services, events, investments | Develop each stream systematically |
| **Geographic diversification** | Local, regional, national, international funding | Expand carefully with capacity |
| **Sector diversification** | Human services, education, environment, arts | Leverage existing expertise |
| **Time diversification** | Year-round vs seasonal fundraising | Stagger campaigns and events |

## Resource Dependence Theory

### Core Proposition
- Organizations depend on environment for resources
- Resource scarcity creates dependence on external sources
- Managers seek to reduce dependence and secure resources
- Dependence + uncertainty = power imbalance

### Strategies to Manage Dependence
1. **Resource diversification**: Multiple funding sources
2. **Environmental scanning**: Monitor trends and changes
3. **Power building**: Develop capacity, reputation, relationships
4. **Vertical integration**: Develop own revenue-generating services
5. **Coalition building**: Partner with other organizations to increase bargaining power

## Financial Sustainability Models

### 1. Fee-Based Model (Earned Income)
- **Concept**: Primary revenue from fees for services/programs
- **Example**: Community college tuition, museum admissions, certification fees
- **Pros**: Reduces donor dependence; enhances credibility; market validation
- **Cons**: May limit access for underserved populations; revenue volatility

### 2. Donor-Dependent Model
- **Concept**: Primary revenue from donations/grants
- **Example**: Major humanitarian relief organizations, some religious organizations
- **Pros**: Maximizes mission flexibility; can serve needs regardless of market
- **Cons**: Vulnerable to donor changes; fundraising pressure; uncertainty

### 3. Hybrid Model (Most Recommended)
- **Concept**: Balanced mix of earned income and contributed income
- **Example**: Universities (tuition + endowments + donations), hospitals (services + insurance + donations)
- **Pros**: Risk distributed; mission flexibility; financial resilience
- **Cons**: Requires sophisticated management; complexity in reporting

### 4. Endowment Model
- **Concept**: Large principal invested; annual spending funds operations
- **Example**: Major universities, hospitals, some large foundations
- **Pros**: Perpetual funding; investment income provides stable support
- **Cons**: Requires significant capital to start; spending rate must be carefully calibrated

## Budgeting and Financial Planning

### Operating Budget
- **Revenue projection**: Based on realistic forecasts of all income sources
- **Expense planning**: Program costs, administrative, fundraising
- **Surplus/deficit planning**: Ensure mission can continue in either scenario
- **Regular review**: Monthly/quarterly comparison of budget vs actual

### Cash Flow Management
- **Timing mismatches**: Revenue may come sporadically; expenses are regular
- **Reserve funds**: Maintain operating reserves (3-6 months recommended)
- **Lines of credit**: Establish relationships before needed
- **Accounts receivable**: Manage government grant payment delays

### Restricted vs Unrestricted Funds
- **Restricted**: Donor-specified purpose; must be used as donor intended
- **Unrestricted**: Board-determined or general operations; most flexible
- **Best practices**: Clearly track and report; avoid accidental commingling
- **Transparency**: Report breakdown in financial statements and to donors

## Economic Impact Measurement

### Outputs vs Outcomes
- **Outputs**: Direct products/services delivered (e.g., "500 students enrolled")
- **Outcomes**: Changes resulting from outputs (e.g., "students improve test scores by 15%")
- **Impact**: Long-term, broader changes in community/society

### Common Metrics by Sector

| Sector | Key Output Metrics | Key Outcome Metrics |
|---|---|---|
| **Education** | Graduation rates, enrollment | Skill acquisition, employment rates |
| **Health** | Services delivered, patients reached | Health improvements, quality of life |
| **Human Services** | People served, meals provided | Housing stability, employment outcomes |
| **Arts/Culture** | Attendance, participants | Community engagement, economic impact |
| **Environment** | Acres conserved, trees planted | Air/water quality improvements, behavioral changes |

### Financial Ratios and Benchmarks

| Ratio | Formula | Typical Benchmark | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Expense Ratio** | Program expenses ÷ Total expenses | < 75% (varies by sector) | Lower = more efficient (but not at expense of mission) |
| **Program Efficiency** | Program services ÷ Total expenses | 70-85% | Indicates proportion supporting mission |
| **Fundraising Efficiency** | Fundraising costs ÷ Funds raised | < $0.30 per $1 raised | Varies by method and organization maturity |
| **Revenue Diversification** | Herfindahl index or number of sources | > 3-5 significant sources | More diversified = more stable |
| **Reserve Ratio** | Unrestricted cash ÷ Monthly operating expenses | 3-6 months | Measures liquidity and risk tolerance |

## Common Economic Pitfalls

### 1. Over-Reliance on Single Funding Source
- **Risk**: Vulnerable to changes in that source
- **Example**: Organization losing major government grant
- **Fix**: Develop diversification strategy with 3-5 year timeline

### 2. Unrestricted Fund Shortage
- **Risk**: Cannot cover overhead, salaries, unexpected costs
- **Fix**: deliberately cultivate unrestricted donations; distinguish restricted vs unrestricted in reporting

### 3. unrealistic Revenue Projections
- **Risk**: Budget deficits; program cuts; staff layoffs
- **Fix**: Conservative forecasting; build in contingency; monthly variance analysis

### 4. Mixing Restricted and Unrestricted Funds
- **Risk**: Compliance issues; donor disputes; audit findings
- **Fix**: Strong accounting systems; clear policies; regular training

### 5. Underinvesting in Financial Management
- **Risk**: Poor decision-making; compliance failures; sustainability threats
- **Fix**: Invest in finance staff/skills; regular financial reporting; board financial literacy

## Strategic Funding Recommendations

### For New Organizations (Years 1-3)
1. **Focus on developing earned income streams** alongside grant fundraising
2. **Build individual donor base** for stability and unrestricted support
3. **Secure diverse government grants** but don't over-rely on any one
4. **Begin endowment planning** even if not immediately feasible
5. **Track all metrics from day one** to establish benchmarks

### For Established Organizations (Years 3+)
1. **Formalize diversification strategy** with specific targets
2. **Develop social enterprise** initiatives related to mission
3. **Endowment building** if feasible for scale and longevity
4. **Regular board financial education** and oversight
5. **Impact investing** exploration if aligned with mission and capacity

### For All Organizations
1. **Diversify before you need it** — don't wait for a crisis
2. **Maintain 3-6 months operating reserves** as liquidity buffer
3. **Report financial health** transparently to donors and stakeholders
4. **Align funding strategies** with strategic plan and mission priorities
5. **Invest in financial management capacity** proportionate to organization size and complexity
