## Course Description

Conduction, convection, and radiation heat transfer mechanisms. This interdisciplinary course connects **mechanical, chemical, civil, and aerospace engineering** to understand thermal systems. Students will learn through **interactive simulations, real-world case studies, and design challenges**.

### Learning Outcomes

Upon completing this course, students will be able to:
1. Analyze heat conduction in solids
2. Understand convection heat transfer
3. Calculate radiation heat exchange
4. Design heat exchangers
5. Apply to real-world thermal systems

---

## Real-World Examples

### Aerospace Engineering

**Example 1: Space Shuttle Thermal Protection**
The Space Shuttle's tiles protected it from 1,650°C during re-entry. Understanding heat transfer helped engineers design these life-saving systems.

**Example 2: Rocket Engine Cooling**
Rocket engines operate at extreme temperatures. Regenerative cooling channels use fuel to cool the engine, preventing meltdown.

### Mechanical Engineering

**Example 3: Car Engine Cooling**
Car engines waste 30% of fuel energy as heat. Radiators remove this heat to keep engines running safely.

**Example 4: Computer Cooling**
Modern CPUs generate 100+ watts of heat in a small area. Heat sinks, fans, and liquid cooling systems are essential for computer performance.

### Chemical Engineering

**Example 5: Heat Exchangers**
Chemical plants use thousands of heat exchangers. Understanding heat transfer helps optimize energy efficiency and process performance.

**Example 6: Insulation Design**
Proper insulation saves energy and money. Understanding heat transfer helps select the right insulation for buildings, pipes, and equipment.

### Civil Engineering

**Example 7: Building Energy Efficiency**
Buildings use 40% of energy. Understanding heat transfer helps design energy-efficient buildings with proper insulation, windows, and HVAC systems.

**Example 8: Fire Protection**
Fire resistance ratings depend on heat transfer. Understanding thermal properties helps design fire-resistant structures.

### Environmental

**Example 9: Climate Change**
Heat transfer affects climate. Understanding radiation and convection helps model global warming and design sustainable systems.

**Example 10: Solar Energy**
Solar panels convert sunlight to electricity. Understanding heat transfer helps optimize solar collector design.

## Story: The Heat Equation

> **Hook**: "In 1822, Joseph Fourier published 'The Analytical Theory of Heat' - a book that changed how we understand thermal systems."

> **Story**: Fourier developed the heat equation to describe how temperature changes in solids. His methods were so powerful that they're now used in many fields beyond heat transfer - from finance to quantum mechanics.

> **Connection**: Every time you use a heat sink, insulation, or a heat exchanger, you're applying Fourier's principles. Understanding heat transfer helps us design better thermal systems.

## Lesson Structure

### Lesson 01: Introduction to Heat Transfer

**Duration:** 50 minutes

**Hook**: "Heat flows from hot to cold - but understanding how fast and through what paths is the key to thermal engineering."

**Story": "In the 1800s, engineers struggled with steam engine efficiency. Understanding heat transfer helped them design better engines and industrial processes."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Define heat transfer and its mechanisms
- Understand temperature and heat
- Learn Fourier's law
- Apply to simple problems

**Content Outline:**

#### 1. What is Heat Transfer? (10 min)
- Definition and scope
- Three mechanisms: conduction, convection, radiation
- Applications

#### 2. Temperature and Heat (12 min)
- Temperature scales
- Heat transfer vs. temperature
- Energy balance

#### 3. Fourier's Law (10 min)
- Heat flux
- Thermal conductivity
- Heat equation

#### 4. Interactive Elements (8 min)
- **Heat Flow Visualizer**: See heat movement
- **Thermal Conductivity Chart**: Compare materials
- **Temperature Calculator**: Solve heat problems

**Real-World Application**: Designing insulation for a building

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate heat flux through a wall.
2. Compare thermal conductivities of materials.
3. Explain the difference between heat and temperature.
4. Design insulation for a given R-value.
5. Calculate heat loss through a window.

### Lesson 02: Conduction - Steady State

**Duration:** 60 minutes

**Hook**: "Steady-state conduction means temperature doesn't change with time. It's the simplest case but covers many real applications."

**Story": "In 1822, Fourier developed the mathematical theory of heat conduction. His work is still the foundation of thermal engineering."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Apply Fourier's law to 1D problems
- Calculate thermal resistance
- Analyze composite walls
- Understand shape factors

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Building Design**: Wall insulation
- **Electronics**: Heat sink design
- **Industrial**: Furnace design
- **Cryogenics**: Vacuum insulation

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate heat transfer through a single wall.
2. Find thermal resistance of a composite wall.
3. Design insulation for a pipe.
4. Calculate heat loss through a composite wall.
5. Design a heat sink for a CPU.

### Lesson 03: Conduction - Transient

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Transient conduction means temperature changes with time. It's important for heating, cooling, and thermal shock."

**Story": "In the 19th century, engineers struggled with thermal stress in steam engines. Understanding transient heat transfer helped them design more durable engines."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand transient heat transfer
- Apply lumped capacitance method
- Use Heisler charts
- Analyze thermal shock

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Manufacturing**: Heat treatment
- **Cooking**: Food processing
- **Electronics**: Startup behavior
- **Fire Protection**: Structural response

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate cooling time using lumped capacitance.
2. Use Heisler charts for transient conduction.
3. Explain thermal shock and how to prevent it.
4. Design a heat treatment process.
5. Calculate the temperature of a heated object.

### Lesson 04: Convection - Fundamentals

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Convection is heat transfer by fluid motion. It's how your radiator heats a room and how your car engine stays cool."

**Story": "In 1701, Isaac Newton proposed the law of cooling - convective heat transfer is proportional to temperature difference. This simple law is the foundation of convection analysis."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand convection mechanisms
- Apply Newton's law of cooling
- Understand boundary layers
- Learn about Nusselt number

**Real-World Applications:**
- **HVAC**: Heating and cooling systems
- **Electronics**: Air and liquid cooling
- **Industrial**: Process heating
- **Automotive**: Engine cooling

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate convective heat transfer coefficient.
2. Explain the difference between natural and forced convection.
3. Calculate heat transfer from a hot surface.
4. Design a convection cooling system.
5. Explain the role of boundary layers.

### Lesson 05: External Flow

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook**: "External flow is when fluid flows over a surface - like air over a car or water over a ship. Understanding this helps us design efficient vehicles."

**Story": "In 1904, Ludwig Prandtl developed boundary layer theory, explaining how fluid flows over surfaces. His work revolutionized aerodynamics and heat transfer."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Analyze flow over flat plates
- Calculate heat transfer for cylinders and spheres
- Understand flow separation
- Apply correlations for different geometries

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Aerospace**: Aircraft cooling
- **Automotive**: Heat exchangers
- **Power Generation**: Cooling towers
- **Environmental**: Heat dissipation

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate heat transfer from a flat plate.
2. Analyze flow over a cylinder.
3. Design a cooling fin.
4. Calculate heat loss from a pipe.
5. Explain flow separation and its effects.

### Lesson 06: Internal Flow

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook": "Internal flow is when fluid flows inside a pipe or duct. It's essential for heat exchangers and HVAC systems."

**Story": "In 1939, Sieder and Tate developed correlations for internal flow heat transfer. Their work is still used today in heat exchanger design."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand laminar and turbulent internal flow
- Apply Nusselt number correlations
- Analyze thermal entry region
- Design pipe systems

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Heat Exchangers**: Shell and tube, plate
- **HVAC**: Duct systems
- **Chemical Engineering**: Process piping
- **Power Generation**: Cooling systems

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate heat transfer in laminar pipe flow.
2. Analyze turbulent flow heat transfer.
3. Design a heat exchanger.
4. Calculate pressure drop and heat transfer.
5. Explain the thermal entry region.

### Lesson 07: Natural Convection

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Natural convection occurs without external forces - hot air rises, cold air falls. It's how buildings heat and cool passively."

**Story": "In the 18th century, scientists studied how heat rises. Their work led to the design of chimneys, radiators, and passive solar buildings."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand natural convection mechanisms
- Apply Grashof and Rayleigh numbers
- Calculate natural convection heat transfer
- Design passive systems

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Building Design**: Passive heating/cooling
- **Electronics**: Natural convection cooling
- **Solar Energy**: Solar chimneys
- **Cooking**: Ovens, slow cookers

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate natural convection from a vertical plate.
2. Explain the difference between laminar and turbulent natural convection.
3. Design a passive cooling system.
4. Calculate heat loss from a hot surface.
5. Explain the stack effect in buildings.

### Lesson 08: Boiling and Condensation

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook**: "Boiling and condensation are phase-change processes with very high heat transfer rates. They're essential for power plants and refrigeration."

**Story": "In 1934, Rohsenow developed the first correlation for pool boiling. His work is still the basis for boiling heat transfer analysis."

- Understand boiling mechanisms
- Analyze pool and flow boiling
- Understand condensation mechanisms
- Apply to real systems

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Power Generation**: Boilers, condensers
- **Refrigeration**: Evaporators, condensers
- **Chemical Engineering**: Distillation
- **Cooking**: Boiling, steaming

**Practice Problems:**
1. Explain the boiling curve.
2. Calculate heat transfer for pool boiling.
3. Design a boiler for a power plant.
4. Analyze film condensation.
5. Design a condenser for a refrigeration system.

### Lesson 09: Radiation Fundamentals

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Radiation transfers heat through electromagnetic waves. It doesn't require a medium - it's how the sun heats the Earth."

**Story": "In 1884, Stefan-Boltzmann discovered that radiation heat transfer is proportional to T⁴. This relationship is fundamental to understanding radiation."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand radiation fundamentals
- Apply Stefan-Boltzmann law
- Understand emissivity and absorptivity
- Calculate radiation exchange

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Solar Energy**: Solar collectors
- **Furnace Design**: High-temperature processes
- **Space**: Satellite thermal control
- **Fire Protection**: Radiation hazards

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate radiation heat transfer from a surface.
2. Explain emissivity and how to measure it.
3. Design a solar collector.
4. Calculate radiation exchange between surfaces.
5. Explain the greenhouse effect.
