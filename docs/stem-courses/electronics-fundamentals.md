## Course Description

Circuit analysis, semiconductor devices, and electronic systems. This interdisciplinary course connects **electrical engineering, computer engineering, physics, and applied mathematics** to understand electronics. Students will learn through **interactive circuit simulations, real-world case studies, and design challenges**.

### Learning Outcomes

Upon completing this course, students will be able to:
1. Analyze DC and AC circuits
2. Understand semiconductor device operation
3. Design amplifier circuits
4. Analyze digital logic circuits
5. Apply electronics to real-world systems

## Real-World Examples

### Consumer Electronics

**Example 1: Smartphones**
A smartphone contains billions of transistors on a single chip. Understanding electronics helps us design faster, more efficient devices.

**Example 2: LED Lighting**
LEDs are 80% more efficient than incandescent bulbs. Understanding semiconductor physics helps us design better lighting systems.

### Power Systems

**Example 3: Solar Inverters**
Solar panels produce DC electricity, but homes use AC. Inverters convert DC to AC using power electronics. Understanding electronics helps us design efficient solar systems.

**Example 4: Electric Vehicles**
Electric motors and batteries require sophisticated power electronics. Understanding motor control and battery management is essential for EV design.

### Communication

**Example 5: Radio and TV**
Radio waves carry information through the air. Understanding modulation and demodulation helps us design communication systems.

**Example 6: Fiber Optics**
Fiber optic cables transmit data at the speed of light. Understanding optical electronics helps us design high-speed networks.

### Medical

**Example 7: Medical Imaging**
MRI, CT, and X-ray machines use complex electronics. Understanding electronics helps us design better diagnostic equipment.

**Example 8: Pacemakers**
Pacemakers regulate heartbeats using electronic circuits. Understanding low-power electronics is essential for implantable devices.

### Industrial

**Example 9: Automation**
Factory automation uses sensors, controllers, and actuators. Understanding electronics helps us design efficient production systems.

**Example 10: Robotics**
Robots use electronics for sensing, processing, and actuation. Understanding electronics is essential for robotics engineering.

## Story: The Transistor Revolution

> **Hook**: "In 1947, three scientists at Bell Labs invented the transistor - a device that would change the world more than any invention since the printing press."

> **Story**: John Bardeen, Walter Brattain, and William Shockley created the first transistor. It was the size of a pinhead but could do what vacuum tubes the size of light bulbs could do. This invention led to computers, phones, and the digital age.

> **Connection**: Today, billions of transistors fit on a single chip. Understanding how transistors work is the key to understanding modern electronics.

## Lesson Structure

### Lesson 01: Introduction to Electronics

**Duration:** 50 minutes

**Hook**: "Electronics is the study of electrical circuits containing active components - transistors, diodes, and integrated circuits."

**Story": "In the early 1900s, electronics meant vacuum tubes - fragile, power-hungry glass devices. The invention of the transistor in 1947 revolutionized electronics."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Define electronics and its applications
- Understand voltage, current, and resistance
- Learn Ohm's law and power law
- Apply basic circuit analysis

**Content Outline:**

#### 1. What is Electronics? (10 min)
- Definition and scope
- Electronic vs. electrical
- Applications

#### 2. Circuit Fundamentals (12 min)
- Voltage, current, resistance
- Ohm's law: V = IR
- Power: P = VI

#### 3. Circuit Analysis (10 min)
- Series and parallel circuits
- Kirchhoff's laws
- Equivalent resistance

#### 4. Interactive Elements (8 min)
- **Circuit Builder**: Create and test circuits
- **Ohm's Law Calculator**: Calculate V, I, R
- **Power Calculator**: Calculate power dissipation

**Real-World Application**: Designing a simple LED circuit

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate current through a 100 Ω resistor with 5 V across it.
2. Find equivalent resistance of 100 Ω and 200 Ω in parallel.
3. Design a voltage divider to get 3 V from a 9 V battery.
4. Calculate power dissipated by a 1 kΩ resistor with 10 mA current.
5. Build a circuit with 3 LEDs in series.

---

### Lesson 02: DC Circuit Analysis

**Duration:** 60 minutes

**Hook**: "DC circuits have constant voltage and current. They're the foundation of all electronic systems."

**Story": "In 1827, Georg Ohm published his law relating voltage, current, and resistance. He was fired from his teaching job for his 'heretical' ideas, but his work became the foundation of electrical engineering."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Apply Kirchhoff's voltage and current laws
- Analyze circuits with multiple sources
- Understand Thevenin and Norton equivalents
- Solve complex DC circuits

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Power Systems**: DC distribution
- **Electronics**: Biasing circuits
- **Measurement**: Sensor interfaces
- **Battery Systems**: Portable devices

**Practice Problems:**
1. Apply KVL to a multi-loop circuit.
2. Find Thevenin equivalent of a circuit.
3. Solve a circuit using mesh analysis.
4. Design a current source using a voltage source and resistors.
5. Calculate power delivered by each source.

---

### Lesson 03: AC Circuit Analysis

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Most electronics use AC - alternating current. Understanding AC is essential for power systems and communication."

**Story": "In the 1880s, Thomas Edison and Nikola Westinghouse fought the 'War of Currents' - DC vs. AC. AC won because it can be easily transformed to different voltages."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand AC waveforms and phasors
- Analyze circuits with resistors, capacitors, and inductors
- Calculate impedance and phase angle
- Apply AC analysis to real circuits

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Power Systems**: AC distribution
- **Audio Systems**: Signal processing
- **Radio**: Tuning circuits
- **Filters**: Frequency selection

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate impedance of an RLC circuit.
2. Find the phase angle between voltage and current.
3. Design a low-pass filter.
4. Calculate power in an AC circuit.
5. Design a resonant circuit for 100 MHz.

---

### Lesson 04: Capacitors and Inductors

**Duration:** 60 minutes

**Hook**: "Capacitors store energy in electric fields; inductors store energy in magnetic fields. Together, they're the building blocks of filters and oscillators."

**Story": "In 1745, Pieter van Musschenbroek invented the Leyden jar - the first capacitor. It could store enough electricity to give a powerful shock."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand capacitor and inductor behavior
- Calculate energy storage
- Analyze RC and RL circuits
- Apply to timing and filtering circuits

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Timing Circuits**: Clocks, delays
- **Filters**: Signal conditioning
- **Energy Storage**: Power supplies
- **Tuning**: Radio circuits

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate the time constant of an RC circuit.
2. Design a timer with 1 second delay.
3. Calculate energy stored in a 100 μF capacitor at 12 V.
4. Design a filter to pass frequencies above 1 kHz.
5. Explain why capacitors block DC but pass AC.

---

### Lesson 05: Diodes

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Diodes are one-way valves for electricity. They're essential for converting AC to DC and protecting circuits."

**Story": "In 1906, Greenleaf Whittier Pickard invented the crystal diode - a semiconductor device that could detect radio signals. This was the beginning of semiconductor electronics."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand diode operation
- Analyze diode circuits
- Apply to rectification and regulation
- Understand LED operation

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Power Supplies**: AC to DC conversion
- **Protection**: Reverse polarity protection
- **Lighting**: LEDs
- **Solar**: Photovoltaic cells

**Practice Problems:**
1. Explain how a diode allows current in one direction.
2. Design a half-wave rectifier.
3. Calculate the output of a full-wave bridge rectifier.
4. Design a simple voltage regulator using a Zener diode.
5. Calculate the current through an LED.

---

### Lesson 06: Transistors - BJT

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook": "Transistors are the building blocks of modern electronics. They can switch, amplify, and process signals."

**Story": "The first transistor was the size of a pinhead. Today, billions fit on a single chip. This miniaturization has enabled computers, phones, and the digital age."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand BJT operation
- Analyze biasing circuits
- Design amplifier circuits
- Understand switching applications

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Amplifiers**: Audio, radio
- **Switches**: Digital circuits
- **Sensors**: Temperature, light
- **Power**: Voltage regulation

**Practice Problems:**
1. Explain how a BJT amplifies current.
2. Design a common-emitter amplifier.
3. Calculate the gain of a transistor amplifier.
4. Design a transistor switch for a motor.
5. Explain the difference between NPN and PNP transistors.

---

### Lesson 07: Transistors - MOSFET

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook**: "MOSFETs are the most common transistors in modern electronics. They're used in computers, phones, and power systems."

**Story": "In 1959, Mohamed Atalla and Dawon Kahng invented the MOSFET. It was smaller, faster, and used less power than the BJT, making modern integrated circuits possible."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand MOSFET operation
- Analyze MOSFET circuits
- Design digital switches
- Compare with BJTs

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Digital Logic**: Computers, phones
- **Power Electronics**: Motor control
- **Amplifiers**: Audio, RF
- **Memory**: DRAM, flash

**Practice Problems:**
1. Explain how a MOSFET works.
2. Design a MOSFET switch for a 12 V motor.
3. Calculate the drain current for a given gate voltage.
4. Compare MOSFET and BJT advantages.
5. Design a simple MOSFET amplifier.

---

### Lesson 08: Operational Amplifiers

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook**: "Op-amps are versatile building blocks. They can amplify, filter, and process signals with just a few resistors and capacitors."

**Story": "In 1963, Robert Widlar designed the first integrated circuit op-amp. It was the size of a fingernail but could do what racks of vacuum tubes could do."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand ideal op-amp characteristics
- Analyze inverting and non-inverting amplifiers
- Design active filters
- Apply to signal processing

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Audio Systems**: Preamplifiers, mixers
- **Instrumentation**: Sensor interfaces
- **Filters**: Active filters
- **Computers**: Analog computers

**Practice Problems:**
1. Design an inverting amplifier with gain of -10.
2. Design a non-inverting amplifier with gain of 5.
3. Calculate the output of a summing amplifier.
4. Design a low-pass active filter at 1 kHz.
5. Design a differential amplifier.

### Lesson 09: Power Supplies

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook": "Power supplies convert AC to DC and regulate voltage. They're essential for almost all electronic devices."

**Story": "Early radios used batteries, which were expensive and unreliable. The invention of the vacuum tube rectifier made household radios practical."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand rectification and filtering
- Design linear regulators
- Understand switching regulators
- Apply to real systems

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Consumer Electronics**: Phone chargers, laptop adapters
- **Industrial**: Power distribution
- **Automotive**: Alternator, voltage regulation
- **Renewable Energy**: Solar inverters

**Practice Problems:**
1. Design a full-wave bridge rectifier with filter.
2. Calculate ripple voltage for a given capacitor.
3. Design a linear regulator using an op-amp.
4. Explain the advantages of switching regulators.
5. Design a power supply for a 5 V digital circuit.

### Lesson 10: Digital Electronics

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook**: "Digital electronics uses binary numbers to process information. Every computer, phone, and digital device is built on digital electronics."

**Story": "In 1947, Claude Shannon showed how Boolean algebra could implement logic circuits. This insight launched the digital revolution."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand number systems (binary, hex)
- Design logic gates
- Analyze combinational circuits
- Understand sequential circuits

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Computers**: Processors, memory
- **Communication**: Encoding, decoding
- **Control**: Digital controllers
- **Displays**: LED, LCD drivers

**Practice Problems:**
1. Convert decimal 42 to binary.
2. Design a half-adder using logic gates.
3. Design a full-adder from half-adders.
4. Explain the difference between combinational and sequential logic.
5. Design a 4-bit counter.

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Sensors convert physical quantities to electrical signals. Interfacing sensors to electronics is essential for measurement and control."

**Learning Objectives:**

- Design sensor interfaces

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Automotive**: Engine sensors, parking sensors

2. Explain how an accelerometer works.

4. Calculate the output of a Wheatstone bridge.
5. Design a sensor system for a weather station.
