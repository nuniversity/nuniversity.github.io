## Course Description

Behavior of fluids at rest and in motion. This interdisciplinary course connects **civil, mechanical, chemical, and biomedical engineering** to understand fluid behavior. Students will learn through **interactive simulations, real-world case studies, and design challenges**.

### Learning Outcomes

Upon completing this course, students will be able to:
1. Apply fluid statics principles
2. Understand fluid dynamics fundamentals
3. Analyze pipe flow and open channel flow
4. Calculate drag and lift forces
5. Design fluid systems

---

## Real-World Examples

### Civil Engineering

**Example 1: Dam Design**
Dams must withstand hydrostatic pressure that increases with depth. The Hoover Dam is 660 feet thick at the base because water pressure is enormous at that depth.

**Example 2: Water Distribution**
City water systems must deliver water at adequate pressure to all buildings. Understanding pipe flow helps engineers design efficient distribution networks.

### Mechanical Engineering

**Example 3: Aerodynamics**
Race cars use aerodynamic principles to generate downforce, improving grip. Understanding drag and lift helps design faster, more efficient vehicles.

**Example 4: HVAC Systems**
Air conditioning systems move air through ducts. Understanding fluid flow helps engineers design efficient systems that minimize energy use.

### Chemical Engineering

**Example 5: Pipeline Transport**
Oil pipelines transport thousands of barrels per day. Understanding pressure drop and pump requirements is essential for pipeline design.

**Example 6: Mixing Processes**
Chemical reactors require proper mixing for efficient reactions. Understanding turbulence and mixing helps optimize reactor design.

### Biomedical Engineering

**Example 7: Blood Flow**
Blood flows through arteries and veins. Understanding fluid dynamics helps diagnose cardiovascular diseases and design medical devices.

**Example 8: Respiratory Systems**
Air flows through lungs and airways. Understanding fluid flow helps design ventilators and respiratory treatments.

### Environmental

**Example 9: River Engineering**
Rivers carry sediment and shape landscapes. Understanding open channel flow helps manage floods and restore ecosystems.

**Example 10: Ocean Currents**
Ocean currents affect climate and marine life. Understanding fluid dynamics helps predict weather and manage coastal areas.

## Story: The Bernoulli Effect

> **Hook**: "In 1738, Daniel Bernoulli published a book that explained how airplanes fly - 165 years before the Wright brothers' first flight."

> **Story**: Bernoulli showed that as fluid speed increases, pressure decreases. This principle explains how airplane wings generate lift: air moves faster over the top of the wing, creating lower pressure above.

> **Connection**: Every time you fly, you're experiencing Bernoulli's principle. Understanding this simple relationship helps engineers design better aircraft, race cars, and wind turbines.

## Lesson Structure

### Lesson 01: Introduction to Fluid Mechanics

**Duration:** 50 minutes

**Hook**: "Fluids are everywhere - air, water, oil, blood. Understanding how they behave is essential for engineering."

**Story": "Archimedes discovered the principle of buoyancy while taking a bath. He was so excited that he ran naked through the streets shouting 'Eureka!' (I have found it!)"

**Learning Objectives:**
- Define fluids and their properties
- Understand density, specific gravity, and viscosity
- Learn Pascal's and Archimedes' principles
- Apply fluid properties to real problems

**Content Outline:**

#### 1. What are Fluids? (10 min)
- Definition and examples
- Liquids vs. gases
- Continuum assumption

#### 2. Fluid Properties (12 min)
- Density and specific weight
- Viscosity
- Surface tension

#### 3. Pascal's and Archimedes' Principles (10 min)
- Pressure transmission
- Buoyancy and floatation

#### 4. Interactive Elements (8 min)
- **Buoyancy Simulator**: Test floating objects
- **Viscosity Comparison**: See how fluids flow differently
- **Pressure Calculator**: Calculate hydrostatic pressure

**Real-World Application**: Designing a boat hull

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate the density of a fluid given mass and volume.
2. Explain why steel ships float.
3. What is the difference between absolute and kinematic viscosity?
4. Calculate the buoyant force on a 1 m³ object submerged in water.
5. Design a floating device to support a 100 kg person.

### Lesson 02: Fluid Statics

**Duration:** 60 minutes

**Hook**: "When fluid is at rest, it exerts pressure on everything it touches. Understanding this pressure is essential for designing dams, tanks, and submarines."

**Story": "In 1648, Blaise Pascal invented the hydraulic press, demonstrating that pressure applied to a fluid is transmitted undiminished. This principle is used in car brakes, hydraulic lifts, and airplane controls."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Calculate hydrostatic pressure
- Determine forces on submerged surfaces
- Analyze manometers and barometers
- Understand buoyancy and stability

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Civil Engineering**: Dam design, tank design
- **Marine Engineering**: Submarine design
- **Hydraulics**: Lifts, brakes
- **Meteorology**: Barometric pressure

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate pressure at 10 m depth in water.
2. Find the force on a dam wall 20 m wide at 15 m depth.
3. Design a manometer to measure 50 kPa pressure.
4. Determine if a floating body is stable.
5. Design a hydraulic press to lift 10 tons with 100 N input force.

---

### Lesson 03: Fluid Kinematics

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Fluid kinematics describes how fluids move - without considering why they move."

**Story": "In the 18th century, Leonhard Euler developed equations describing fluid motion. His work laid the foundation for modern fluid dynamics."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand flow visualization
- Describe velocity and acceleration fields
- Classify flows (steady/unsteady, laminar/turbulent)
- Apply continuity equation

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Aerospace**: Wind tunnel testing
- **Civil Engineering**: River flow analysis
- **Environmental**: Pollution dispersion
- **Manufacturing**: Process flow

**Practice Problems:**
1. Draw streamlines for flow around a cylinder.
2. What is the difference between Lagrangian and Eulerian descriptions?
3. Classify each flow: steady/unsteady, laminar/turbulent.
4. Apply continuity equation to find exit velocity.
5. Design a flow visualization experiment.

### Lesson 04: Bernoulli's Equation

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook**: "Bernoulli's equation relates pressure, velocity, and elevation in a flowing fluid. It explains how airplanes fly and how carburetors work."

**Story": "Daniel Bernoulli discovered that as fluid velocity increases, pressure decreases. This counterintuitive result is the basis of aerodynamics."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Derive and apply Bernoulli's equation
- Understand its limitations
- Solve practical problems
- Apply to real-world systems

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Aerospace**: Airfoil design
- **Civil Engineering**: Flow measurement
- **Automotive**: Carburetors, fuel injection
- **Sports**: Curve balls, golf ball dimples

**Practice Problems:**
1. Apply Bernoulli's equation to a tank with a hole.
2. Calculate the velocity of water exiting a pipe.
3. Explain how a Venturi meter works.
4. Why does a curveball curve?
5. Design a flow meter using Bernoulli's principle.

### Lesson 05: Momentum Equation

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "The momentum equation tells us how fluids exert forces on objects - essential for designing pipes, bends, and propulsion systems."

**Story": "In 1784, Euler applied Newton's second law to fluids, developing the momentum equation. This allowed engineers to calculate forces in fluid systems."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Apply momentum equation to control volumes
- Calculate forces in pipes and bends
- Analyze jet impact
- Understand thrust and propulsion

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Hydraulics**: Pipe design, force calculations
- **Aerospace**: Rocket propulsion
- **Civil Engineering**: Dam forces
- **Manufacturing**: Jet cutting

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate the force on a pipe bend.
2. Find the thrust of a rocket engine.
3. Determine the force of a jet hitting a plate.
4. Design a fire hose nozzle.
5. Calculate the force needed to hold a pipe in place.

### Lesson 06: Energy Equation

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook": "The energy equation accounts for losses in real fluid systems - friction, turbulence, and minor losses."

**Story": "In 1843, Julius Weisbach developed the Darcy-Weisbach equation for pipe friction. This equation is still used today to design pipe systems."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Apply energy equation to fluid systems
- Calculate major and minor losses
- Design pipe systems
- Optimize pump selection

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Water Supply**: Distribution systems
- **Oil & Gas**: Pipeline design
- **HVAC**: Duct systems
- **Industrial**: Process piping

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate head loss in a 100 m pipe.
2. Design a pipe system for a given flow rate and pressure.
3. Select a pump for a water distribution system.
4. Calculate the effect of fitting losses on flow.
5. Optimize a pipe system for minimum energy.

### Lesson 07: Laminar Flow

**Duration:** 60 minutes

**Hook**: "Laminar flow is smooth, orderly fluid motion - like honey flowing slowly. It's predictable and easy to analyze."

**Story": "In 1839, Gotthilf Hagen and Jean Léonard Marie Poiseuille independently studied laminar flow in pipes. Their work led to the Hagen-Poiseuille equation."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand laminar flow characteristics
- Apply Hagen-Poiseuille equation
- Analyze flow between plates
- Calculate shear stress

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Biomedical**: Blood flow in capillaries
- **Lubrication**: Bearing design
- **Microfluidics**: Lab-on-a-chip
- **Viscometry**: Fluid property measurement

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate flow rate in a pipe with laminar flow.
2. What is the velocity profile in laminar pipe flow?
3. Calculate the pressure drop for a given flow rate.
4. Design a microfluidic device.
5. Measure viscosity using a capillary viscometer.

---

### Lesson 08: Turbulent Flow

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Turbulent flow is chaotic and unpredictable - like whitewater rapids. Most engineering flows are turbulent."

**Story": "In 1883, Osborne Reynolds demonstrated the transition from laminar to turbulent flow. His famous dye experiment showed that turbulence occurs above a critical Reynolds number."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand turbulent flow characteristics
- Apply Moody chart for friction factor
- Calculate turbulent flow properties
- Understand Reynolds number

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Civil Engineering**: River flow, dam design
- **Aerospace**: Boundary layer control
- **Chemical Engineering**: Mixing, reactors
- **Environmental**: Pollution dispersion

**Practice Problems:**
1. Determine if a flow is laminar or turbulent.
2. Use the Moody chart to find friction factor.
3. Calculate head loss in turbulent pipe flow.
4. Design a flow to stay laminar.
5. Explain the Reynolds number physically.

### Lesson 09: External Flow

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook**: "External flow is when fluid flows around objects - airplanes, cars, buildings. Understanding drag and lift is essential for design."

**Story": "In 1904, Ludwig Prandtl developed boundary layer theory, explaining how drag works. His work made modern aerodynamics possible."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand boundary layers
- Calculate drag and lift forces
- Analyze flow around objects
- Apply to real-world design

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Aerospace**: Aircraft design
- **Automotive**: Car aerodynamics

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate drag on a sphere.

5. Design a building to withstand wind loads.

### Lesson 10: Open Channel Flow

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Open channel flow is when fluid flows with a free surface - rivers, canals, spillways. It's essential for water resource engineering."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand open channel flow characteristics
- Apply Chezy and Manning equations
- Analyze critical flow
- Design channels and spillways

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Civil Engineering**: Canal design, flood control
- **Environmental**: River management
- **Agriculture**: Irrigation systems
- **Hydropower**: Dam spillways

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate flow rate in a rectangular channel.
2. Determine critical depth for a given flow.
3. Design a channel to carry a given flow.
4. Analyze flow over a weir.
5. Calculate flood stage in a river.

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook**: "Compressible flow occurs when fluid density changes significantly - like in jet engines and supersonic flight."

**Story": "In 1947, Chuck Yeager broke the sound barrier in the Bell X-1. Understanding compressible flow made supersonic flight possible."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand Mach number and compressibility
- Analyze isothermal and isentropic flow
- Study normal shock waves
- Apply to high-speed systems

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Aerospace**: Supersonic and hypersonic flight
- **Gas Dynamics**: Nozzle design

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate Mach number for given conditions.
2. Analyze flow through a converging-diverging nozzle.
3. What happens at Mach 1?
4. Design a supersonic inlet.
5. Calculate conditions across a normal shock.
