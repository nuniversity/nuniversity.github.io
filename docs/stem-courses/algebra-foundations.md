## Course Description

A comprehensive introduction to algebraic thinking, covering fundamental concepts from basic operations to polynomial functions. This course builds the mathematical foundation essential for all STEM disciplines. Students will develop fluency in algebraic manipulation, equation solving, and function analysis through **interactive visualizations, real-world applications, and storytelling**.

### Learning Outcomes

Upon completing this course, students will be able to:
1. Perform operations with real numbers, variables, and expressions
2. Solve linear equations and inequalities in one and two variables
3. Graph and analyze linear functions
4. Solve systems of linear equations
5. Understand and apply quadratic functions

## Real-World Examples

### Budgeting & Finance

**Example 1: Monthly Budget**
You earn $x per hour and work h hours per week. Your monthly income is:
```
Income = x × h × 4
```
If you earn $15/hour and work 20 hours/week:
```
Income = 15 × 20 × 4 = $1,200/month
```

**Example 2: Savings Goal**
You want to save $5,000 for a car. You save $200/month. How many months?
```
5000 = 200 × months
months = 5000 / 200 = 25 months
```

### Cooking & Recipes

**Example 3: Recipe Scaling**
A recipe serves 8 people, but you need to serve 12. If the recipe calls for 3 cups of flour:
```
Flour needed = 3 × (12/8) = 4.5 cups
```

**Example 4: Unit Conversion**
A recipe uses teaspoons, but you only have tablespoons. Since 1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons:
```
Tablespoons = Teaspoons / 3
```

### Sports & Fitness

**Example 5: Running Pace**
If you run 5 miles in 45 minutes, your pace is:
```
Pace = 45 minutes / 5 miles = 9 minutes per mile
```

**Example 6: Calorie Burn**
If you burn C calories per minute and exercise for t minutes:
```
Total calories = C × t
```

## Story: The Algebra Detective

> **Hook**: "What if I told you that algebra was invented by a Persian mathematician who was solving inheritance problems 1,200 years ago?"

> **Story**: In 820 AD, Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi wrote "The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing." His work on solving equations was so influential that the word "algorithm" comes from his name. He was solving real problems: how to divide inheritance according to Islamic law, how to calculate land areas, how to determine canal dimensions.

> **Connection**: Every time you solve an equation, you're using techniques that al-Khwarizmi developed to solve real-world problems over a millennium ago.

## Lesson Structure

### Lesson 01: What is Algebra?

**Duration:** 45 minutes

**Hook**: "What if I told you that the phone in your pocket contains more computing power than NASA had for the Moon landing? And all of it runs on algebra."

**Story**: In 1969, Margaret Hamilton was working on the Apollo guidance software when she realized the computer might try to do too many tasks at once. She invented "asynchronous software" - and the algebra behind it saved the Apollo 11 mission.

**Learning Objectives:**
- Define algebra and its role in mathematics
- Identify variables, constants, and coefficients
- Translate between words and algebraic expressions
- Understand the real number system

**Content Outline:**

#### 1. Introduction to Algebra (10 min)
- What is algebra? Why do we need it?
- History of algebra (Al-Khwarizmi, origins)
- Real-world applications of algebra

#### 2. Numbers and Operations Review (10 min)
- Real number system (natural, integer, rational, irrational)
- Order of operations (PEMDAS)
- Properties of operations (commutative, associative, distributive)

#### 3. Variables and Expressions (15 min)
- What is a variable?
- Constants vs. variables
- Coefficients and terms
- Writing algebraic expressions from words

#### 4. Interactive Elements (10 min)
- **Number Line Explorer**: Drag points on number line, identify number types
- **Expression Builder**: Drag-and-drop to build expressions
- **Quiz**: 5 questions on terminology

**Real-World Application**: Budgeting - If you earn $12/hour and work h hours, your pay is 12h

**Practice Problems:**
1. Identify the variable, constant, and coefficient in: 4x + 7
2. Write an algebraic expression for "5 more than a number"
3. Classify each number as rational or irrational: -3, 0.5, √2, 7/3
4. Simplify using order of operations: 3 + 4 × 2
5. Use the distributive property: 3(x + 4)

**Assessment:** 5-question quiz (must score 80% to proceed)

### Lesson 02: Numbers & Operations

**Duration:** 50 minutes

**Hook**: "How many times have you been at the store and tried to figure out if you have enough money? That's algebra in action."

**Story**: "A grocery store manager needs to calculate discounts. If a product costs $P and is on sale for 20% off, the sale price is 0.80P. But she also needs to calculate tax, which is 8%. So the final price is 1.08 × 0.80P = 0.864P. That's 13.6% off the original price!"

**Learning Objectives:**
- Perform operations with integers, fractions, and decimals
- Apply order of operations to complex expressions
- Convert between fractions, decimals, and percents
- Use scientific notation

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Shopping**: Calculating discounts and tax
- **Finance**: Interest rates, loan payments
- **Science**: Scientific notation for very large/small numbers
- **Cooking**: Scaling recipes up/down

**Practice Problems:**
1. Simplify: (-3) × 4 + 2 × (-5)
2. Add: 3/4 + 2/3
3. Convert 0.75 to a fraction
4. What is 15% of 240?
5. Write 0.000045 in scientific notation

### Lesson 03: Variables & Expressions

**Duration:** 55 minutes

**Hook**: "Variables are like characters in a story - they can represent anything, and we can make them do amazing things."

**Story**: "In computer programming, variables are everywhere. When you type your name into a form, the computer stores it in a variable called 'name'. When you play a game and your score increases, that's a variable called 'score'. The algebra you learn here is the foundation of all programming."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Combine like terms
- Use the distributive property
- Simplify algebraic expressions
- Evaluate expressions for given values

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Programming**: Variables in code
- **Science**: Formulas for distance, speed, time
- **Business**: Revenue = Price × Quantity
- **Health**: BMI = weight(kg) / height(m)²

**Practice Problems:**
1. Combine like terms: 3x + 5x - 2x + 7
2. Expand: 4(2x - 3) + 5
3. Simplify: 2(x + 3) - 4(x - 1)
4. Evaluate 2x² - 3x + 1 when x = 2
5. Simplify: 3(2a + b) - 2(a - 3b)

### Lesson 04: Equations & Inequalities

**Duration:** 60 minutes

**Hook**: "An equation is like a balanced scale. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other to keep it balanced."

**Story**: "A detective arrives at a crime scene. There are two unknowns: the speed of the getaway car and the time it had been traveling. The detective knows the total distance was 120 miles and the car left at 2:00 PM, arriving at 4:00 PM. Using algebra, the detective can figure out the car was traveling at 60 mph."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Solve one-variable linear equations
- Solve one-variable linear inequalities
- Translate word problems into equations
- Check solutions

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Business**: Break-even analysis
- **Medicine**: Dosage calculations
- **Engineering**: Design specifications
- **Everyday**: "How much can I spend?"

**Practice Problems:**
1. Solve: 3x + 7 = 22
2. Solve: 2(x - 4) = 3x + 1
3. Solve and graph: 2x - 5 > 7
4. Write and solve: A number increased by 8 is 15
5. Solve: 4 - 3(x + 2) = -2

### Lesson 05: Introduction to Functions

**Duration:** 55 minutes

**Hook**: "A function is like a machine. You put something in, something comes out. The same input always gives the same output."

**Story**: "Netflix uses functions to recommend movies. Your viewing history is the input, and the function analyzes it to output recommendations. The function considers: genre preferences, viewing time, ratings, and similar users' preferences."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Define a function
- Identify domain and range
- Use function notation
- Determine if a relation is a function

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Technology**: Algorithms, data processing
- **Science**: Mathematical models
- **Business**: Revenue functions
- **Everyday**: Distance = Speed × Time

**Practice Problems:**
1. Is this a function? {(1,2), (2,3), (3,4), (1,5)}
2. Find f(3) if f(x) = 2x - 1
3. State the domain: f(x) = √(x - 2)
4. Use the vertical line test on the given graph
5. Find the range of f(x) = x²

### Lesson 06: Linear Functions

**Duration:** 60 minutes

**Hook**: "Linear functions are everywhere - your salary, your phone bill, the distance you travel."

**Story**: "A taxi company charges $3 as a base fare plus $2 per mile. The function C(m) = 2m + 3 tells you the cost for m miles. If you travel 10 miles, it costs 2(10) + 3 = $23."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Graph linear functions
- Identify slope from graph and equation
- Write equations in slope-intercept form
- Interpret slope and y-intercept

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Business**: Cost functions, revenue models
- **Science**: Linear relationships in experiments
- **Everyday**: Distance-time graphs
- **Finance**: Simple interest calculations

**Practice Problems:**
1. Find the slope through (2, 3) and (4, 7)
2. Graph y = 2x - 3
3. Write the equation with slope 4 and y-intercept -2
4. Convert 3x + 2y = 12 to slope-intercept form
5. Find the slope and y-intercept from the graph

### Lesson 07: Systems of Equations

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Sometimes one equation isn't enough. When you have two unknowns, you need two equations."

**Story**: "A farmer has 100 animals: chickens and cows. He counts 280 legs total. How many of each? Chickens have 2 legs, cows have 4. We can write: c + w = 100 and 2c + 4w = 280. Solving gives us 60 chickens and 40 cows."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Solve systems graphically
- Solve systems using substitution
- Solve systems using elimination
- Identify number of solutions

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Business**: Product mix optimization
- **Science**: Balancing equations
- **Engineering**: Circuit analysis
- **Everyday**: Comparing phone plans

**Practice Problems:**
1. Solve by graphing: y = 2x + 1, y = -x + 4
2. Solve by substitution: y = 3x - 2, 2x + y = 8
3. Solve by elimination: 2x + 3y = 12, 4x - 3y = 6
4. How many solutions? 2x + 4y = 8, x + 2y = 4
5. Write and solve: Two numbers add to 10, differ by 4

### Lesson 08: Quadratic Functions

**Duration:** 60 minutes

**Hook**: "When you throw a ball, it follows a parabola. That's a quadratic function in action."

**Story**: "In 1609, Galileo discovered that objects fall in parabolic paths. This was revolutionary - before Galileo, people thought heavier objects fell faster. His experiments showed that all objects follow the same quadratic curve, regardless of weight."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Graph parabolas
- Identify vertex, axis of symmetry, and intercepts
- Convert between standard and vertex form
- Solve quadratics by factoring

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Physics**: Projectile motion
- **Engineering**: Parabolic antennas
- **Economics**: Profit maximization
- **Sports**: Optimal throwing angles

**Practice Problems:**
1. Graph y = x² - 4x + 3
2. Find the vertex of y = 2(x + 1)² - 5
3. Factor: x² - 5x + 6
4. Solve by factoring: x² - 7x + 12 = 0
5. Convert to vertex form: y = x² + 6x + 8

### Lesson 09: Polynomials

**Duration:** 55 minutes

**Hook**: "Polynomials are like algebra's building blocks - they make up almost every function you'll encounter."

**Story**: "Your phone's GPS uses polynomial equations to calculate your position. The satellite signals travel at the speed of light, and polynomials help convert the time delays into accurate coordinates."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Classify polynomials by degree and terms
- Perform operations with polynomials
- Factor polynomials completely
- Apply polynomial functions

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Computer Science**: Polynomial algorithms
- **Engineering**: Signal processing
- **Physics**: Trajectory calculations
- **Economics**: Revenue/cost models

**Practice Problems:**
1. Add: (3x² + 2x - 1) + (x² - 4x + 5)
2. Multiply: (2x + 3)(x - 4)
3. Factor completely: 2x² + 5x - 3
4. Find all zeros: x³ - 4x² + x + 6 = 0
5. Find the area of a rectangle with sides (x + 3) and (2x - 1)

- Rationalize denominators

**Real-World Applications:**

**Practice Problems:**

2. Add: 2√3 + 3√3
3. Multiply: √6 × √10
4. Rationalize: 3/√5
5. Solve: √(x + 3) = 5
