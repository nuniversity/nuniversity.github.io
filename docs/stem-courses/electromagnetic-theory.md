## Course Description

Electric and magnetic fields, Maxwell's equations, and electromagnetic waves. This interdisciplinary course connects **electrical engineering, physics, telecommunications, and applied mathematics** to understand electromagnetism. Students will learn through **interactive field visualizations, real-world case studies, and design challenges**.

### Learning Outcomes

Upon completing this course, students will be able to:
1. Understand electric and magnetic fields
2. Apply Gauss's and Ampère's laws
3. Understand Maxwell's equations
4. Analyze electromagnetic wave propagation
5. Apply to antennas, waveguides, and transmission lines

## Real-World Examples

### Telecommunications

**Example 1: Cell Phones**
Cell phones communicate using electromagnetic waves. Understanding antenna design and wave propagation helps us build better communication networks.

**Example 2: WiFi and Bluetooth**
WiFi uses 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz radio waves. Understanding electromagnetic theory helps us design faster, more reliable wireless networks.

### Power Systems

**Example 3: Transformers**
Transformers use electromagnetic induction to change voltage levels. Understanding transformer design is essential for power distribution.

**Example 4: Electric Motors**
Electric motors convert electrical energy to mechanical energy. Understanding magnetic fields helps us design more efficient motors.

### Medical

**Example 5: MRI**
Magnetic Resonance Imaging uses strong magnetic fields and radio waves to create detailed images of the body. Understanding electromagnetism is essential for MRI design.

**Example 6: Radiation Therapy**
Radiation therapy uses electromagnetic radiation to treat cancer. Understanding dose distribution helps us design effective treatments.

### Space

**Example 7: Solar Flares**
Solar flares emit intense electromagnetic radiation. Understanding space weather helps us protect satellites and power grids.

**Example 8: GPS**
GPS uses electromagnetic signals from satellites. Understanding signal propagation helps us improve accuracy.

### Industrial

**Example 9: Welding**
Electric welding uses high current to melt metal. Understanding electromagnetic heating helps us design better welding processes.

**Example 10: Induction Heating**
Induction heating uses magnetic fields to heat metal. It's used in cooking, manufacturing, and heat treatment.

## Story: Maxwell's Equations

> **Hook**: "In 1865, James Clerk Maxwell published four equations that unified electricity, magnetism, and light. Einstein called this the most important event in physics since Newton."

> **Story**: Maxwell showed that electric and magnetic fields are two aspects of the same phenomenon - electromagnetism. His equations predicted electromagnetic waves traveling at the speed of light, proving that light is an electromagnetic wave.

> **Connection**: Every wireless device - from radios to smartphones to GPS - is based on Maxwell's equations. Understanding these equations helps us design better communication and sensing systems.

## Lesson Structure

### Lesson 01: Introduction to Electromagnetism

**Duration:** 50 minutes

**Hook**: "Electricity and magnetism are two faces of the same coin - electromagnetism."

**Story": "In 1820, Hans Christian Ørsted discovered that a compass needle deflects when near a current-carrying wire. This was the first evidence that electricity and magnetism are related."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand electric and magnetic fields
- Learn Coulomb's and Biot-Savart's laws
- Apply to simple problems
- Understand field superposition

**Content Outline:**

#### 1. What is Electromagnetism? (10 min)
- Electric and magnetic fields
- Historical perspective
- Applications

#### 2. Electric Fields (12 min)
- Coulomb's law
- Electric field lines
- Superposition principle

#### 3. Magnetic Fields (10 min)
- Magnetic force on charges
- Biot-Savart law
- Ampère's law

#### 4. Interactive Elements (8 min)
- **Field Visualizer**: See electric and magnetic fields
- **Coulomb's Law Calculator**: Calculate forces
- **Field Line Plotter**: Draw field lines

**Real-World Application**: Designing an electrostatic precipitator

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate the electric field from a point charge.
2. Find the force between two charges.
3. Draw field lines for a dipole.
4. Calculate the magnetic field from a current-carrying wire.
5. Explain why lightning follows the path it does.

### Lesson 02: Electric Potential

**Duration:** 60 minutes

**Hook**: "Electric potential is the 'voltage' that drives current. Understanding potential helps us design circuits and understand field behavior."

**Story": "In 1780, Luigi Galvani discovered that touching frog legs with different metals caused them to twitch. This led to the invention of the battery."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Define electric potential and potential energy
- Calculate potential from charge distributions
- Understand equipotential surfaces
- Apply to real problems

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Circuit Design**: Voltage sources
- **Capacitors**: Energy storage
- **Particle Accelerators**: Potential difference
- **Lightning**: Potential breakdown

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate potential from a point charge.
2. Find potential difference between two points.
3. Draw equipotential surfaces for a dipole.
4. Calculate the energy stored in a capacitor.
5. Design a Van de Graaff generator.

---

### Lesson 03: Gauss's Law

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Gauss's law relates electric flux to enclosed charge. It's one of the most powerful tools in electromagnetism."

**Story": "Carl Friedrich Gauss developed his law in 1835. It's so elegant that it's one of Maxwell's four equations."

**Learning Objectives:**
- State Gauss's law
- Apply to symmetric charge distributions
- Understand electric flux
- Solve problems using Gauss's law

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Capacitor Design**: Parallel plate, cylindrical
- **Electrostatic Shielding**: Faraday cages
- **Lightning Rods**: Charge concentration
- **Particle Detection**: Ionization chambers

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate the field inside a charged sphere.
2. Find the field from an infinite line charge.
3. Design a Faraday cage.
4. Calculate flux through a surface.
5. Explain why lightning rods work.

---

### Lesson 04: Conductors and Capacitors

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook**: "Conductors allow charge to flow freely. Capacitors store charge. Together, they're the foundation of electronic circuits."

**Story": "In 1745, Pieter van Musschenbroek invented the Leyden jar - the first capacitor. It could store enough electricity to give a powerful shock."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand conductor properties
- Analyze capacitors (parallel plate, cylindrical, spherical)
- Calculate capacitance
- Understand energy storage

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Power Supplies**: Filtering, energy storage
- **Touchscreens**: Capacitive sensing
- **Memory**: DRAM, flash
- **Timing**: Clock circuits

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor.
2. Find the energy stored in a capacitor.
3. Design a capacitor for a specific application.
4. Explain why conductors shield interior fields.
5. Calculate the capacitance of a cylindrical capacitor.

---

### Lesson 05: Current and Resistance

**Duration:** 60 minutes

**Hook**: "Current is the flow of charge. Resistance opposes current flow. Together, they determine how circuits behave."

**Story": "In 1827, Georg Ohm published his law relating voltage, current, and resistance. He was initially ridiculed, but his work became the foundation of electrical engineering."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand current density and conductivity
- Apply Ohm's law in microscopic form
- Calculate resistance of materials
- Understand temperature effects

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Wire Design**: Size selection
- **Resistors**: Current limiting
- **Heating**: Resistance heating
- **Sensors**: Thermistors, strain gauges

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate current density in a wire.
2. Find the resistance of a copper wire.
3. Explain why power lines use high voltage.
4. Design a heating element.
5. Calculate the effect of temperature on resistance.

### Lesson 06: Kirchhoff's Laws

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook": "Kirchhoff's laws are the foundation of circuit analysis. They're applications of conservation of charge and energy."

**Story": "In 1845, Gustav Kirchhoff formulated his laws while still a student. They allowed him to analyze circuits that were previously impossible to solve."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Apply Kirchhoff's current law (KCL)
- Apply Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL)
- Solve complex circuits
- Use mesh and nodal analysis

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Circuit Analysis**: Complex networks
- **Power Systems**: Load flow
- **Electronics**: Biasing networks
- **Troubleshooting**: Fault finding

**Practice Problems:**
1. Apply KCL to a node with multiple currents.
2. Apply KVL around a loop.
3. Solve a circuit using mesh analysis.
4. Solve a circuit using nodal analysis.
5. Design a circuit to meet specific requirements.

### Lesson 07: Magnetic Fields and Forces

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook": "Magnetic fields exert forces on moving charges and current-carrying wires. This is the principle behind electric motors and generators."

**Story": "In 1820, André-Marie Ampère immediately grasped the significance of Ørsted's discovery and developed the mathematical theory of electromagnetism within weeks."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand magnetic field sources
- Calculate forces on charges and wires
- Apply Lorentz force law
- Understand magnetic materials

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Motors**: Converting electrical to mechanical energy
- **Generators**: Converting mechanical to electrical
- **Magnetic Levitation**: Trains, bearings
- **MRI**: Medical imaging

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate the force on a charge moving in a magnetic field.
2. Find the force on a current-carrying wire.
3. Explain how an electric motor works.
4. Design a simple motor.
5. Calculate the magnetic field inside a solenoid.

### Lesson 08: Electromagnetic Induction

**Duration:** 70 minutes

**Hook**: "A changing magnetic field creates an electric field. This is electromagnetic induction - the principle behind generators and transformers."

**Story": "In 1831, Michael Faraday discovered electromagnetic induction. He showed that moving a magnet through a coil creates voltage - the principle behind all electric generators."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand Faraday's law
- Calculate induced EMF
- Understand Lenz's law
- Apply to real problems

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Power Generation**: Generators
- **Transformers**: Voltage conversion
- **Induction Heating**: Cooking, manufacturing
- **Wireless Charging**: Phone chargers

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate induced EMF from a changing magnetic field.
2. Explain Lenz's law with an example.
3. Design a transformer for a given voltage ratio.
4. Calculate the EMF in a rotating coil.
5. Explain how wireless charging works.

### Lesson 09: Maxwell's Equations

**Duration:** 75 minutes

**Hook**: "Maxwell's four equations unify electricity, magnetism, and light. They're the most important equations in physics."

**Story": "Maxwell's equations predicted electromagnetic waves before they were discovered. When Heinrich Hertz confirmed their existence in 1887, Maxwell's theory was proven correct."

**Learning Objectives:**
- State and interpret Maxwell's equations
- Understand displacement current
- Derive electromagnetic wave equation
- Calculate wave properties

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Communication**: Radio, TV, WiFi
- **Radar**: Detection and ranging
- **Optics**: Light propagation
- **Remote Sensing**: Satellite imaging

**Practice Problems:**
1. State each of Maxwell's equations and explain its meaning.
2. Derive the wave equation from Maxwell's equations.
3. Calculate the speed of light from ε₀ and μ₀.
4. Explain the relationship between E and B fields in a wave.
5. Calculate the wavelength of a 100 MHz radio wave.

4. Design an antenna for a given frequency.
5. Explain how radio waves propagate around the Earth.

### Lesson 11: Transmission Lines

**Duration:** 65 minutes

**Hook": "Transmission lines carry high-frequency signals efficiently. They're essential for power distribution and communication."

**Story": "In the 1880s, Oliver Heaviside developed transmission line theory. His work made long-distance telephony and power transmission possible."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand transmission line theory
- Calculate characteristic impedance
- Analyze reflections
- Match impedance for maximum power transfer

**Real-World Applications:**
- **Power Distribution**: Long-distance transmission
- **Telecommunication**: Coaxial cables, fiber optics
- **RF Engineering**: Antenna feeds
- **High-Speed Digital**: PCB traces

**Practice Problems:**
1. Calculate the characteristic impedance of a coaxial cable.
2. Explain why impedance matching is important.
3. Design a matching network.
4. Calculate the reflection coefficient.
5. Explain why high-frequency signals need special treatment.
