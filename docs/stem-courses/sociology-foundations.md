## Course Description

A deep, comprehensive exploration of society, social behavior, and social structures. This course presents sociology as the **science of society** - connecting individual behavior to larger social patterns through interactive simulations and engaging case studies.

### Learning Outcomes

Upon completing this course, students will be able to:
1. Understand sociological thinking and research methods
2. Explore socialization and identity formation
3. Analyze social institutions and structures
4. Understand social inequality and stratification
5. Explore social change and movements
6. Apply sociological perspectives to real-world issues

---

## Real-World Examples

### Social Structure

**Example 1: Social Class**
Social class affects opportunities, health, and life outcomes. Understanding social stratification helps us address inequality.

**Example 2: Family Structures**
Family structures vary across cultures and time. Understanding these variations helps us appreciate diverse family forms.

### Social Interaction

**Example 3: Social Norms**
Social norms guide behavior. Understanding how norms form and change helps us understand social order and deviance.

**Example 4: Roles and Status**
We all play multiple roles - student, parent, employee. Understanding roles helps us navigate social life.

### Social Inequality

**Example 5: Gender Inequality**
Gender roles and expectations affect opportunities and outcomes. Understanding gender stratification helps us promote equality.

**Example 6: Racial and Ethnic Inequality**
Racial and ethnic groups face different opportunities. Understanding these disparities helps us address discrimination.

### Social Change

**Example 7: Social Movements**
Social movements drive change. Understanding how movements form and succeed helps us create positive social change.

**Example 8: Globalization**
Globalization connects people worldwide. Understanding its effects helps us navigate a connected world.

### Research Methods

**Example 9: Survey Research**
Surveys help us understand public opinion. Designing good surveys is essential for accurate data.

**Example 10: Ethnography**
Ethnography studies cultures through immersion. Understanding ethnographic methods helps us appreciate diverse perspectives.

## Story: The Sociological Imagination

> **Hook": "Sociology is the study of society - how we live together, how we organize ourselves, and how we change."

> **Story": "C. Wright Mills (1959) coined the term 'sociological imagination' - the ability to see the connection between personal troubles and public issues. This perspective helps us understand that individual problems often have social causes."

> **Connection**: Every concept you learn in this course is a tool for understanding society. By developing your sociological imagination, you gain the ability to see the larger patterns that shape your life.

## Lesson Structure

### Lesson 01: What is Sociology?

**Duration:** 50 minutes

**Hook**: "Sociology is the scientific study of society. It helps us understand why people behave the way they do and how social structures shape our lives."

**Story": "Auguste Comte (1798-1857) coined the term 'sociology' and argued that society could be studied scientifically. Émile Durkheim, Max Weber, and Karl Marx further developed the discipline."

**Learning Objectives:**
- Define sociology and its scope
- Understand sociological perspectives
- Learn research methods
- See how sociology connects to everyday life

**Content Outline:**

#### 1. What is Sociology? (10 min)
- Definition and scope
- Relationship to other sciences
- The sociological imagination

#### 2. Sociological Perspectives (12 min)
- Structural functionalism
- Conflict theory
- Symbolic interactionism

#### 3. Research Methods (10 min)
- Surveys
- Ethnography
- Experiments
- Secondary data analysis

#### 4. Interactive Elements (8 min)
- **Sociology Explorer**: Explore different perspectives
- **Research Method Simulator**: Design studies
- **Sociological Imagination Activity**: Connect personal and public

**Real-World Application**: Understanding social issues, policy analysis

**Practice Problems:**
1. What is the sociological imagination?
2. How does structural functionalism view society?
3. What is the difference between a variable and a constant?
4. Give an example of how sociology is used in everyday life.
5. What are the ethical issues in sociological research?
